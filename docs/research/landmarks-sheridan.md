# Town landmarks (Sheridan) — research notes (13 November 2026)
Status: draft

## Summary (≤ 10 lines: what the coding agent needs to know)
Sheridan is split north–south by the South Yamhill River. The only street crossing in town is Sheridan Bridge (1939), a mint-green steel Parker through-truss 258 ft (78.6 m) long on Bridge Street. The denser historic commercial core is on **South Bridge Street** (rebuilt in brick after the 18 July 1913 fire), not a long Main Street canyon; Main Street (OR 18 Business) runs east–west on the **north** side of the river and meets Bridge Street at the town’s traffic signal. OSM is missing most of Sheridan’s downtown buildings, so these facades have to be hand-made (TW-5) or filled from another footprint source (TW-4). The former Wilco grain elevator by the tracks — a TW-5 default candidate and once the town skyline — was still standing in 2006–2009, was already partly demolished on 7 November 2013, and is **not visible** on current aerial imagery of the S Bridge / rail crossing. Do not model it as a standing current landmark unless the owner wants a historical or optional version. South of the tracks, Sheridan High School and its running track sit on S Bridge; further south, just off OR 18, FCI Sheridan is a large red-roofed prison campus in open fields. World x,z for individual buildings is unknown. The public minimap label “Sheridan” is at x=3800, z=1400 (`game/src/minimap.js`); that is a town label, not a building point.

## Facts

### Downtown layout (block by block)
- What / where: Sheridan’s commercial district is centred on Bridge Street and Main Street, with their intersection as the location of the city’s traffic signal (Wikipedia, citing older city sources; a 7 November 2013 photo still shows signals at this junction). **Main Street** is Oregon Route 18 Business and runs east–west on the north bank of the South Yamhill River. **Bridge Street** runs north–south, crosses the river on Sheridan Bridge, continues south across the freight railroad, and reaches OR 18 just north of FCI Sheridan. Address points (OpenStreetMap Nominatim): 119 E Main 45.099552°N, 123.394808°W; 129 W Main 45.099501°N, 123.395584°W; 142 S Bridge (museum) 45.099203°N, 123.395072°W; 229 S Bridge (Heider) 45.097922°N, 123.395225°W; 342 S Bridge (former elevator / Dura Lite site) 45.096707°N, 123.395296°W; City Hall at 120 SW Mill St 45.096984°N, 123.395733°W. World x,z: unknown (near the Sheridan minimap label at x=3800, z=1400).
- Size: The attached commercial frontage is short — roughly the block of Main at Bridge, plus about two blocks of S Bridge between the river and the tracks (measure from current aerial; no surveyed block lengths found). Most downtown buildings are one or two storeys. — confidence: high for the street pattern and the 1–2-storey limit; medium for how many lots are still occupied vs parking — source: [Wikipedia, Sheridan, Oregon (Transportation / Economy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan,_Oregon); current ESRI World Imagery of downtown (fetched 13 November 2026); Nominatim (ODbL)
- Materials & colours: After the 1913 fire, the south-side business district was rebuilt in brick and stronger materials. Street-level photos show a mix of red/tan brick two-storey blocks with decorative brick parapets, one-storey painted or stuccoed shop rows with continuous green metal awnings, concrete sidewalks, yellow curb paint, wooden utility poles and dense overhead wires, and hanging flower baskets on some corners. A tree belt runs along both river banks. Away from the Bridge & Main cluster, Main Street opens out: two-lane asphalt, a sidewalk, flags on poles, and trees along the river side rather than a continuous storefront wall. — confidence: high for the post-1913 brick rebuild and for the 2009/2013 streetscape colours; medium for what is unchanged since those photos (Heider was under restoration in 2022) — source: [City of Sheridan, History](https://www.cityofsheridanor.com/history); [Oregon Encyclopedia, Sheridan](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/sheridan/); [Commons: downtown at Bridge and Sheridan roads, 27 August 2009](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_downtown_at_Bridge_and_Sheridan_roads.JPG); [Commons: Oregon Route 18B in Sheridan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oregon_Route_18B_in_Sheridan.JPG)
- Distinctive features: The green through-truss is the visual hinge of downtown. Commercial buildings meet both ends of the bridge. The J. A. Art Hebert Memorial Plaza is on Bridge Street near the river. Sheridan City Park (with a skatepark) is signed from the Bridge & Main junction. The 1913 fire destroyed 50 buildings on the south side; a temporary “Sheridan Arch” on S Bridge, finished in 1912, also burned and should not be modelled as existing.
- Photos: [2009 streetscape looking along the commercial street](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_downtown_at_Bridge_and_Sheridan_roads.JPG) (1–2-storey shops, green awnings, two-storey red brick mid-block, overhead wires, hills beyond); [Bridge Street looking south onto the green truss from the W Main / City Park signs and the traffic signal, during 2013 repairs](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bridge_Street,_Sheridan.jpg); [OR 18B / Main Street along the tree-lined river edge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oregon_Route_18B_in_Sheridan.JPG); [historic South Bridge after the 1913 rebuild](https://www.sheridanmuseumofhistory.com/gallery-5-1); [current aerial of the river, Bridge Street, Main, S Bridge and the tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan,_Oregon) (use also ESRI imagery of the same bbox).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: treating Main Street as a long McMinnville-style brick canyon (it is not); putting the historic two-storey brick row on the north side of the river (the densest post-1913 brick is on **S Bridge**); omitting the river tree belt; adding kerbs if the rest of the game has none (the photos show yellow-painted curb edges and sidewalks). Because OSM has almost no downtown footprints, a generic extrusion pass will leave this street empty.

### Sheridan Bridge (South Yamhill River Bridge, Bridge No. 02557)
- What / where: Two-lane steel through-truss carrying Bridge Street over the South Yamhill River. North end is the intersection with Main Street (OR 18 Business); the south end leads into the S Bridge commercial blocks. The only highway/street river crossing inside the city (a railroad bridge crosses further upriver). Maintained by Yamhill County. Coordinates: 45.098912°N, 123.395433°W (Wikipedia); Living New Deal gives 45.099029°N, 123.39519°W. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Total length 258 ft (78.6 m); width 26 ft (7.9 m); two traffic lanes plus sidewalks on both sides. Opened 28 June 1939 (construction 1938–39). Height of the truss above the deck: unknown (no sourced figure). — confidence: high for length, width, date, and type — source: [Wikipedia, Sheridan Bridge (Oregon)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan_Bridge_(Oregon)); [Oregon Historic Sites Database, site 19921](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=v.dsp_siteSummary&resultDisplay=19921); [1994 City of Sheridan inventory form (SHPO scan)](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=19921.pdf)
- Materials & colours: Riveted steel Parker Pratt through-truss on concrete anchors. After the 2013 ODOT-funded retrofit (seismic work, new paint, deck resurfacing) the steel is a light mint / sage green. Galvanized steel traffic guardrails on the inside of the sidewalks; green pedestrian railings with concrete posts. Approach lamps are historic-style gray/black posts with white globes (the 1994 inventory notes Art Deco lamp posts and steel railing with Art Deco lines on the posts). Concrete deck. — confidence: high for the 2013 green paint and truss form (photographed after the repaint); medium for whether the Art Deco lamps visible in 2013 are the 1939 originals or replacements — source: [Commons, Sheridan Bridge 2013](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Bridge_2013_-_Sheridan,_Oregon.JPG); [Commons category, Sheridan Bridge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Sheridan_Bridge); [Living New Deal, Sheridan Bridge](https://livingnewdeal.org/sites/sheridan-bridge-sheridan/); 1994 inventory form above
- Distinctive features: A complete through-truss the player drives *through*, not just over. The News-Register called the 2013 work the “Green Bridge project.” Built with Public Works Administration (Federal Emergency Administration of Public Works) funds — not WPA (a common error, including in an older Wikipedia sentence). Eligible/significant in the Oregon Historic Sites Database; included in the 2018 Sheridan Downtown reconnaissance-level survey and a 2013 statewide steel-truss survey. A bronze plaque is on the bridge. Living New Deal’s 2022 visit noted collapsed riverbank at the southwest corner.
- Photos: [2013 view looking along the green truss, downtown storefronts at the far end](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Bridge_2013_-_Sheridan,_Oregon.JPG); [2013 front / portal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Bridge_2013_front_-_Sheridan,_Oregon.JPG); [2013 side](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Bridge_2013_side_-_Sheridan,_Oregon.JPG); [from the north during repairs, with the traffic signal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bridge_Street,_Sheridan.jpg); [older Wikipedia image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_Bridge_1.JPG); [Living New Deal site photo](https://livingnewdeal.org/sites/sheridan-bridge-sheridan/); [bridge plaque](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan,_Oregon_Bridge_Plaque.JPG).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a flat or concrete highway bridge; painting it rust-brown or gray instead of mint green; omitting the sidewalks and portal you drive through; placing a second street bridge in town. This is one of the most important handmade pieces in Sheridan.

### Former Wilco grain elevator (by the tracks) — no longer standing
- What / where: A tall metal-clad grain elevator that stood beside the freight railroad at the south edge of downtown, at the S Bridge Street crossing. The petition to save it called it the former Wilco Farmers property (Wilco closed 6 June 2004) and “our only remaining grain tower in the city” after the West Valley Seed Tower burned on 6 March 1992. In 2007 the Oregonian reported that Dura Lite Rock, which owned the elevator, planned to dismantle it. Dun & Bradstreet lists Dura Lite Rock / DLR Services at 342 S Bridge St, Sheridan (45.096707°N, 123.395296°W). A 7 November 2013 photograph captioned “A partially demolished grain elevator” was taken at camera 45.096283°N, 123.396767°W, looking at this site. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Storeys / height / footprint in metres: unknown (no sourced height). It was a 1960s elevator. In the 2006 and 2009 photographs the complete tower, including a flared headhouse and cap, stood several times as tall as nearby two-storey buildings and was the only vertical spike on the town skyline. The 2013 photo shows the cap already gone, leaving a rectangular shaft still several times the height of the adjacent two-storey white building. The Oregonian (18 May 2007) reported a wood skeleton of about 250,000 board feet under aluminum cladding; do not treat that board-foot figure as a building dimension. — confidence: high that it was a 1960s aluminum-over-wood tower and that it dominated the skyline; low for any metric height — source: [The Oregonian, 18 May 2007](https://www.oregonlive.com/breakingnews/2007/05/sheridan_residents_try_to_save.html); [Save The Sheridan Grain Elevator petition](https://www.ipetitions.com/petition/savethegrainelevator/); [Commons, 2006 former Wilco tower](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan,_Oregon_Grain_tower.JPG)
- Materials & colours: Vertical silver / white metal (aluminum) panels. 2006: a narrow shaft with a slightly wider upper headhouse, a small hipped or gabled cap, and two small dark openings stacked in the upper stage; a low white shed in the foreground and railroad rails at the base. 2013 (partial demolition): cap and headhouse gone, ragged open top, the remaining shaft a plain rectangular metal box; a lower attached warehouse/lean-to and a loading spout on one side; chain-link and wire fence; a concrete ramp or dock in the foreground; a white two-storey building with a red metal roof to one side and green-roofed industrial sheds beyond (see Sheridan Building & Feed, below). — confidence: high for the 2006 and 2013 appearance — source: [Commons, 2006](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan,_Oregon_Grain_tower.JPG); [Commons, 7 November 2013, “partially demolished”](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Grain_Elevator.jpg)
- Distinctive features: Was a skyline beacon, visible from OR 18 for miles (2007 reporting and the May 2008 hill photograph). A flag once flew from the top and was lit at night. It stood immediately beside the rails and, in a 27 August 2009 photo, rose directly behind the red-roofed fire station.
- Photos: [2006 complete tower beside the tracks](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan,_Oregon_Grain_tower.JPG); [May 2008 view of Sheridan from a hill, elevator as the central skyline spike](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_hill.JPG); [27 August 2009 fire station with the still-complete tower behind the roof](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_fire_station.JPG); [7 November 2013, cap gone, shaft still standing](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Grain_Elevator.jpg). Current aerial: no tall tower remains at this crossing (ESRI World Imagery, fetched 13 November 2026) — low warehouses, a materials yard, and the rail line only.
- Notes for the game: **Critical for TW-5.** The owner’s default first-three list includes “Sheridan grain elevator.” It is not there now. Modelling a tall silver elevator as current scenery would be historically evocative but factually wrong for a present-day drive. If a historical or “memory” model is wanted, use the 2006 form (flared headhouse and cap), not the 2013 stump. The West Valley Seed Tower (burned 1992) should not be added as a second standing elevator.

### Sheridan City Hall (depot-style building)
- What / where: 120 SW Mill Street, next to the railroad at the south edge of downtown (Nominatim 45.096984°N, 123.395733°W), not in the Main Street retail cluster. Wikipedia’s body text and a 23 January 1993 News-Register headline say the city moved its hall into the renovated train-station depot in January 1993 and enlarged it in 1995; a Wikipedia caption and some later summaries instead call it a former savings-and-loan built in the 1980s to resemble a depot on the depot site. The building itself is clearly designed as a depot, including a station nameboard. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: One storey, a long depot-like range with a lower connecting wing. Length × width × height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for one storey and the elongated plan; low for metric size — source: [City of Sheridan, Contact](https://www.cityofsheridanor.com/contact); [Wikipedia, Sheridan, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan,_Oregon); [Commons, wider view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_city_hall.JPG)
- Materials & colours: Pale gray horizontal clapboard / lap siding; dark burgundy or maroon bargeboards, brackets, window frames and corner boards; brown wood-shingle (or shingle-look) gable roofs. A front-facing gable holds a paired arched window and turned drop ornaments. Ground-floor windows are divided-light units in a bay. A flagpole stands at one end. — confidence: high (2006 close-up and a later wide shot) — source: [Commons, 2006 front](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan,_Oregon_City_Hall.JPG); [Commons, later wide view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_city_hall.JPG)
- Distinctive features: A railroad-style nameboard lettered “SHERIDAN”, with “ELEVATION 207 FEET” (63.1 m) on the left and “TO PORTLAND 57 M.” (91.7 km) on the right. (Official city elevation figures differ: GNIS 197 ft / 60 m. Use 207 ft only as sign lettering.) Hanging flower baskets and a tall ornamental lamp post appear in the wide photograph. Yamhill County Sheriff’s Office has had a West Valley substation at city hall.
- Photos: [2006 close-up of the gable, bay windows and nameboard](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan,_Oregon_City_Hall.JPG); [wider street view of the long gray depot form](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_city_hall.JPG); [historic 1959 depot for comparison, Ben Maxwell / Salem Public Library, via the Oregon Encyclopedia](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/sheridan/).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: placing city hall on Main Street; omitting the “SHERIDAN” nameboard (the most readable facade cue); treating the Wikipedia caption-vs-body disagreement as settled. For the game, model the building as it looks: a one-storey gray-and-maroon depot.

### Heider Building
- What / where: 229 S Bridge Street, at Monroe and Bridge, in the south-side brick row. A century-old two-storey brick commercial building, built about 1917 on the site of a late-19th-century structure destroyed in the 1913 fire. The second floor once held the Otto Heider law firm, with storefronts below. Restored in a project reported 7 July 2022 (Oregon Main Street Network grant). World x,z: unknown. Nominatim: 45.097922°N, 123.395225°W.
- Size: Two storeys. The owner’s company described a 15,000-square-foot (about 1,390 m²) building. Four ground-floor storefronts of about 1,700 square feet (about 158 m²) each, opening onto Bridge Street, with 14 ft (4.3 m) retail ceilings. Eight apartments planned upstairs (studio to three-bedroom) after the retail floor. Footprint length × width and overall height in metres: unknown beyond those area figures. — confidence: high for two storeys, date, and the published areas; medium for whether the apartment finish-out is complete — source: [News-Register, “Heider Building retail space nearing completion,” 7 July 2022](https://newsregister.com/article?articleId=43681); [Museum gallery caption, Heider at Monroe and Bridge](https://www.sheridanmuseumofhistory.com/gallery-5-1)
- Materials & colours: Two-storey brick. The 2022 article says crews touched up crumbling mortar, put on a new roof, replaced windows, and did seismic work. A mid-block two-storey red-brown brick building with a decorative brick cornice and green awnings appears in the 27 August 2009 S Bridge / Sheridan Road streetscape; that is the right type and location, but this research did not get a labelled post-restoration facade photo, so do not treat 2009 paint and awning colours as confirmed for 2026. — confidence: high for brick, two storeys, and the 1917 rebuild; low for current paint, awning colour, and window rhythm after the 2022 work — source: News-Register article; 2009 Commons streetscape above
- Distinctive features: One of the more substantial brick blocks on S Bridge; four equal shop bays; rear apartment entrance and parking planned in 2022.
- Photos: [News-Register 2022 construction photo (freely viewable in the article)](https://newsregister.com/article?articleId=43681); [2009 streetscape that includes the two-storey brick mid-block](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_downtown_at_Bridge_and_Sheridan_roads.JPG); [museum historic photo, Heider at Monroe and Bridge](https://www.sheridanmuseumofhistory.com/gallery-5-1).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a one-storey box, or using the 2009 awning colours without a current check. Ask the owner for a 2022–2026 facade photo before locking trim colours.

### Sackett Building / Sheridan Museum of History
- What / where: 142 S Bridge Street, on the south side of the river, closer to the bridge than the Heider Building (Nominatim 45.099203°N, 123.395072°W). The city and the chamber say the museum is in the restored historic 1914 Sackett Building. The museum opened in the building in February 2018 and purchased it in March 2019. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Construction date 1914. Storeys, footprint and height in metres: unknown (no sourced facade description or measured drawing was found). — confidence: high for the address, 1914 date, and 2018 opening; low for massing — source: [City of Sheridan, Museum of History](https://www.cityofsheridanor.com/index.asp?SEC=C42C5FD7-D2D5-446F-8316-E2241C4DC50F); [Sheridan Chamber, Museum of History](https://sheridanoregonchamber.com/business/sheridan-museum-of-history/); [Museum, About](https://www.sheridanmuseumofhistory.com/about)
- Materials & colours: Unknown from a dated, labelled exterior photo. As a 1914 S Bridge rebuild it is likely brick commercial, but that is an inference from neighbours and should not be used as a fact.
- Distinctive features: A 1914 downtown commercial building now used as a museum / community space. Interior exhibits (schoolhouse, general store, post office, kitchen, fire department) do not affect the exterior.
- Photos: No freely viewable, clearly labelled exterior photo of the Sackett Building was found (the chamber “museum” image is a logo). Historic S Bridge block photos are on the [museum gallery](https://www.sheridanmuseumofhistory.com/gallery-5-1).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: inventing the facade, or confusing this address with the library (which also shows the number 142, on the **north** side of the river at Bridge and Yamhill) or with the Green Frog / former Hi-Way Theater (on Main Street). Owner photos of 142 S Bridge are needed.

### Lee’s Green Frog (former Hi-Way Theater)
- What / where: On Main Street, north of the river. The museum gallery says the Hi-Way Theater was built by M.W. Potter, opened 2 June 1932, and became the Green Frog Restaurant in 1973. Wikimedia’s 28 December 2013 photo is captioned “Green Frog restaurant on Main Street.” A current restaurant listing uses 119 E Main St (Nominatim 45.099552°N, 123.394808°W), just east of Bridge & Main. Cinema Treasures’ older “119 W. Valley Highway” is a less precise historic address for the same highway through town. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Two storeys. Footprint and height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for two storeys and the Main Street location; medium for the exact 119 E Main door — source: [Commons, 28 December 2013](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Green_Frog_-_Sheridan,_Oregon.JPG); [Museum gallery](https://www.sheridanmuseumofhistory.com/gallery-5-1); [Cinema Treasures, Hi-Way Theater](https://www.cinematreasures.org/theaters/32636)
- Materials & colours: Orange-tan brick. A distinctive stepped, ziggurat-like brick parapet rises at the centre, with vertical brick fluting — a 1930s Art Deco theatre cue. Upper-floor windows are single-hung or 1/1 units in pairs, with white shutters (three pairs to the left of the sign in the 2013 photos, more to the right). A continuous green standing-seam metal awning runs the width of the ground-floor storefronts; shop windows have green frames. A hanging “Lee’s Green Frog / American • Chinese” sign sits above a small theatre-style letter board. — confidence: high for the 2013 facade; medium for whether the same awning, shutters and sign remain in 2026 — source: [Commons, Green Frog, 2013](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Green_Frog_-_Sheridan,_Oregon.JPG); [Commons, Lee’s Green Frog](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lee%27s_Green_Frog,_Sheridan.jpg)
- Distinctive features: The stepped central parapet is the most recognisable single storefront in Sheridan. The letter-board is a leftover theatre gesture.
- Photos: the two Commons images above.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a plain brick box without the stepped parapet; placing this building on S Bridge or in the Sackett Building. This facade is worth a handmade model even if other Main Street shops stay generic.

### Huntley Building
- What / where: A one-storey brick commercial block on the north side of downtown. The museum gallery dates it to 1935. Visitor7’s 7 November 2013 photo was taken at 45.099355°N, 123.394636°W (near Main / Yamhill, east of Bridge). World x,z: unknown.
- Size: One storey, with a tall brick parapet. Several shop bays (about five or six in the 2013 photo). Footprint and height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for one storey, 1935 date, and the 2013 appearance — source: [Museum gallery](https://www.sheridanmuseumofhistory.com/gallery-5-1); [Commons, 7 November 2013](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Huntley_Building,_Sheridan.jpg)
- Materials & colours: Tan / brown brick; dark green painted storefronts, transom windows in a band above the shop windows, and a dark green name panel on the parapet lettered “HUNTLEY BUILDING.” A pink/maroon painted neighbour sits to one side. — confidence: high for 2013 — source: same Commons file
- Distinctive features: The parapet lettering is the readable cue. Transom band over the shop windows.
- Photos: [2013 facade](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Huntley_Building,_Sheridan.jpg).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: leaving off the lettered panel, or placing it on S Bridge. Occupancy in the 2013 photo included vacant or papered windows; do not freeze those as “the” current shops.

### Sheridan Public Library

- Size: One storey; 3,000 sq ft (280 m²). Height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for one storey and the published floor area (2000s library statistics cited by Wikipedia) — source: [Wikipedia, Sheridan, Oregon (Culture)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan,_Oregon); [Commons, Sheridan Public Library](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Public_Library.JPG)

### Travelers Home (Savage–Mendenhall–Seth House) — National Register
- What / where: 147 NE Yamhill Street, northwest corner of Yamhill and Faulconer, one block north of Main Street (Nominatim 45.100408°N, 123.394632°W). Built 1892; individually listed on the National Register on 8 July 1982; the only NRHP building in Sheridan as of the 2011 state list. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Two storeys on a hip-roofed main volume, plus a one-storey rear kitchen/dining volume and a lower lean-to on the north. The original town-plat lot was 66 × 132 ft (20.1 × 40.2 m) before a 1929 subdivision; the house occupies the southerly 72 ft (21.9 m) of Lot 8. Height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high (NRHP nomination) — source: [Oregon Historic Sites Database, Travelers Home](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=v.dsp_siteSummary&resultDisplay=19851); [NRHP nomination PDF](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=NR_Noms%2F82003757.pdf)
- Materials & colours: Wood frame, shiplap siding, Italianate with Queen Anne / Eastlake surface decoration. Low hipped roof (original shingles later overlaid with composition shingles) with boxed eaves; a low central facade gable with patterned shingles and a decorative vergeboard. A 12-inch (0.30 m) sawtooth-bottom picket frieze under the eaves. Typical windows are double-hung with coloured border lights in the upper sashes; ground-storey facade windows are wider, with eight coloured panes in the upper sashes. A later Commons photo shows pale mauve / beige siding, gray shutters, cream trim, a central porch with a small upper deck, and a gray picket fence. — confidence: high for the 1982 nomination; medium for current paint (the Commons photo is later and the nomination already noted added shutters) — source: nomination PDF; [Commons, Travelers Home](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Travelers_Home_-_Sheridan_Oregon.jpg)
- Distinctive features: Symmetrical three-bay facade; central gable; coloured border glass; the only building of this type in the original plat.
- Photos: [Commons, spring facade with cherry tree](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Travelers_Home_-_Sheridan_Oregon.jpg).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a generic farmhouse without the central gable, frieze, and coloured upper sashes. This is a quiet landmark one block off Main, not a downtown storefront.

### Sheridan Building & Feed / Building Materials (rail crossing)
- What / where: Green industrial/retail sheds at the railroad crossing on the south edge of downtown, next to the former elevator site (the green buildings at the right of the 2013 elevator photo). A 2013 Commons photo shows railroad crossing signals at the left edge and an “ODOT” street-name sign. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: One-storey metal buildings with gable roofs. Footprints and heights: unknown. — confidence: high for one storey and the crossing location — source: [Commons, Sheridan Building Materials](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Building_Materials.jpg)
- Materials & colours: Green metal wall siding, rusted corrugated metal roofs, a wood-lined recessed entry, a circular “Sheridan Building Materials” sign, a “Sheridan Building & Feed” sign, a red-and-white checkerboard sign, a chain-link yard, and a tall white vertical “PROPANE” tank. — confidence: high for the 2013 photo — source: same
- Distinctive features: The propane tank, the green colour, the rusted roofs, and the rail-crossing signals are all easy player cues at the tracks — the place where a player may expect the elevator.
- Photos: [2013 street view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Building_Materials.jpg); the green sheds also appear at the right of [the 2013 elevator photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Grain_Elevator.jpg).
- Notes for the game: Business names may have changed; the green sheds, tank, and crossing are the modelable forms.

- Size: One storey, several apparatus bays. A stone-clad vertical element (hose or stair tower) rises through the roofline. Metric size: unknown. — confidence: high for the 2009 appearance — source: [Commons, 27 August 2009](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_Oregon_fire_station.JPG)

### Sheridan High School and track
- What / where: On SE Jefferson Street, just southeast of the S Bridge / railroad crossing, east of S Bridge as it heads toward OR 18 (Nominatim 45.095278°N, 123.393889°W). Aerials show a school campus and a running track / football field immediately south of the tracks on the east side of S Bridge. World x,z: unknown.

Metric floor areas and heights: unknown (a 2024 district facilities plan exists but was not fully extracted here). — confidence: high for the location and the track; medium for storey count — source: Nominatim; current aerial of south Sheridan; [Commons, Sheridan High School](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sheridan_High_School.jpg)

### Federal Correctional Institution, Sheridan
- What / where: A federal medium-security prison with an adjacent detention centre and a minimum-security camp, on farmland south of town, opened 1989. Just south of the OR 18 bypass at the south end of Bridge Street. Wikipedia coordinates 45.08389°N, 123.38167°W; Nominatim for the complex 45.083403°N, 123.386459°W, addressed as 27072 SW Ballston Road. The 1986 site approval was 182 acres (74 ha) south of Sheridan. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: A large multi-building campus. The main compound sits inside a rounded-rectangular perimeter road. Published inmate capacity and internal building counts are not needed for a driving game and are omitted here. Building heights in metres: unknown from a primary exterior source; housing units read as low (about two-storey) institutional wings on the public aerials. — confidence: high for location, 1989 opening, and the overall campus plan; low for metric building heights — source: [Wikipedia, Federal Correctional Institution, Sheridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Correctional_Institution,_Sheridan); [Commons overhead](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Federal_Correctional_Institution_Sheridan_overhead_view.jpg)
- Materials & colours: Many buildings have light / white walls and distinctive **red** pitched roofs. A large white-roofed central building sits in the main compound; red-roofed housing wings are arranged in angular or chevron / Y-like plans around curved walks. A second cluster of red- and white-roofed buildings lies west of the main compound. The complex is set in open fields, with large rectangular ponds or reservoirs to the north. — confidence: high for the public aerial appearance — source: same Commons overhead; current ESRI aerial of the site (fetched 13 November 2026)
- Distinctive features: By far the largest building group in Sheridan, and unlike any other place in the county. From OR 18, from the south end of Bridge Street, and especially from the air (jet / glider), the red roofs on a green field are unmistakable. Model only the publicly visible massing (roofs, general footprints, perimeter as a simple fence or tree line).
- Photos: [Wikimedia overhead](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Federal_Correctional_Institution_Sheridan_overhead_view.jpg); Wikipedia also has a front-entrance image on the same article.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: leaving this as empty fields (it is a major Hwy 18 landmark); over-detailing security hardware. Keep it as a large, simple, red-roofed institutional campus.

- Notes for the game: Other 1979-inventory houses and churches (Methodist at 234 N Bridge — the 1875 United Methodist Church was gutted by fire in December 2008; Baptist, Trinity Lutheran, etc.) were not re-verified as current landmarks in this pass. Do not invent their present appearance.

## Which matter most
Ranked by how much a player in a car, on a bike, or in the jet would notice them (not by historic importance alone):

1. **Federal Correctional Institution, Sheridan** — a large red-roofed campus in open fields just south of OR 18; nothing else in the county looks like this from the highway or the air.
2. **Sheridan Bridge** — the only street crossing of the river, a mint-green steel through-truss every player driving through town must pass through.
3. **Bridge Street & Main Street commercial core** — the actual “town”: short blocks of 1–2-storey brick and awning storefronts, especially the Green Frog’s Art Deco stepped parapet and the two-storey brick on S Bridge.
4. **Sheridan High School and track** — a large brick-and-blue campus and a running-track oval on S Bridge between downtown and the highway / prison.
5. **City Hall (depot-style) and the rail crossing** — the gray-and-maroon “SHERIDAN” station building, green feed-store sheds, propane tank and crossing signals; this is where a player expects the grain elevator and instead finds these.

The demolished Wilco elevator would have ranked first or second (it was the skyline). It should not be on a “current landmarks” list unless the owner asks for a historical model.

## Gaps — what you couldn't find, and what to ask the owner
- **Current photos of S Bridge Street**, especially the restored Heider Building and the 1914 Sackett Building (museum at 142 S Bridge). No labelled exterior of the Sackett Building was found.
- **Confirm the elevator site** on a drive or a dated 2024–2026 aerial / Street View: this research used a 2013 “partially demolished” photo plus a current ESRI aerial with no tower. Any remaining slab, pit, or low shed should be noted before TW-5 starts.
- **World x,z** for every site. These notes give WGS84 and the public minimap town label (x=3800, z=1400). Conversion needs `data/raw/naip_info.json` (not in the docs copy).
- **Metric heights** for all buildings except the bridge length/width. Lidar already in the tile pipeline may be the best source for collision tops.
- **2018 Sheridan Downtown RLS** is recorded in the Oregon Historic Sites Database (the bridge record cites it) but the survey PDF was not found online. Worth requesting from SHPO or the city — it should be a block-by-block inventory.
- **1994 City of Sheridan Inventory of Historic Properties** (Peter J. Edwards): only the bridge form was retrieved. Other property forms would help Heider, Sackett, Huntley, the 1914 bank, and the 1916 former city hall on N Bridge.
- **Current occupancy** of older named buildings (Red Lizard / former creamery, 1914 bank, Northside Garage, Chat and Chew). Use building forms, not possibly stale shop names.
- **United Methodist Church** after the December 2008 fire: rebuilt, ruined, or gone?
- **Whether the Bridge & Main traffic signal** and the 2013 green bridge paint are still as photographed.
- **City Hall origin** (true depot vs depot-style 1980s building): model the visible depot form; the owner may know the local story.
