# Town landmarks (Willamina) — research notes (13 November 2026)
Status: draft

## Summary (≤ 10 lines: what the coding agent needs to know)
Willamina is a small timber town on OR 18 Business (Main Street), just north of the OR 18 / OR 22 junction, with a creek/river corridor of trees along the south edge of downtown. Main Street is only a few commercial blocks: mostly plain one-storey shops, plus one distinctive two-storey tan-brick block and the two-storey Wildwood Hotel. North of downtown, along Willamina Creek, the **Hampton Lumber** mill is the largest man-made thing in town — long white-roofed mill buildings, log and lumber yards, and packaged-lumber stacks. The historic Pacific Face Brick plant, once just north of downtown, closed in 1974 and was razed in 1976; do not model it. The 1887 first church now houses the Willamina Historical Museum at 188 NE D Street (no good exterior photo was found). City Hall is a white, house-like gabled building at 411 NE C Street. World x,z for individual buildings is unknown. The public minimap label “Willamina” is at x=-3640, z=3330 (`game/src/minimap.js`); that is a town label, not a building point. Willamina has far more OSM building coverage than Sheridan, so handmade work should concentrate on the mill, the hotel, and the brick Main Street block.

## Facts

### Downtown / Main Street (streetscape)
- What / where: Willamina’s commercial street is Main Street (also described as OR 18 Business in a 2009 Commons caption). The core is only a few blocks of one- and two-storey shops, with parking lots and some empty lots mixed in, and a wooded creek or river corridor immediately south of the commercial strip (current aerial). The official city coordinates are 45.07833°N, 123.48472°W. Willamina Historical Museum at 188 NE D Street is 45.078673°N, 123.482869°W (Nominatim). City Hall at 411 NE C Street was photographed at 45.079769°N, 123.485044°W (Commons GPS, 21 March 2018). World x,z: unknown (near the Willamina minimap label at x=-3640, z=3330).
- Size: A short downtown: a handful of commercial blocks, almost all one storey except the historic brick block and the Wildwood Hotel (two storeys). Street width and typical shop footprints in metres: unknown (measure from aerial). — confidence: high for the short, mostly one-storey pattern — source: current ESRI World Imagery of downtown (fetched 13 November 2026); [Commons, downtown Main Street, 27 August 2009](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Oregon_downtown_Main_Street.JPG); [The Oregonian, “Oregon Offtrack: Willamina,” 11 January 2009](https://www.oregonlive.com/travel/2009/01/oregon_offtrack_willamina.html)
- Materials & colours: The 2009 Oregonian piece described Main Street as “plain, flat buildings” housing a bar, antiques shops, a market and a hardware store. Dated photos show: a one-storey tan / beige painted flat-roofed corner building; a two-storey tan-brick commercial block with maroon window trim (part of the upper facade painted cream/white) and a decorative brick cornice; smaller painted one-storey shops; striped or dark awnings; hanging flower baskets; wooden utility poles and overhead wires; two-lane asphalt with a yellow centre line and parked cars at the curb. A 2009 photo at Main and D Street shows a one-storey light-gray gable building with dark roof and striped awnings, then lower shops and evergreens beyond — the plainer end of the street. — confidence: high for the 2009 streetscape; medium for current paint, awnings and shop names — source: same 2009 Commons photos; [Commons, Main at D Street](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Oregon_Main_at_D_street.JPG)
- Distinctive features: This is not a continuous brick canyon. The two-storey brick block and the Wildwood Hotel are the “old downtown” cues among otherwise modest one-storey sheds and shops. Speed-limit 25 / end-school-zone signs appear at the brick block. A 1961 Salem Public Library photograph of the business district (Coffee Shop, Bunn Insurance, Ellingsworth Grocery, a food locker) is useful as history only.
- Photos: [27 August 2009 Main Street, brick block and tan one-storey neighbour](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Oregon_downtown_Main_Street.JPG); [Main at D Street, plainer one-storey shops](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Oregon_Main_at_D_street.JPG); [Antiques shop close-up of the tan brick block](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Antiques_and_the_Alley_on_Main_Street.jpg); [Skybergs Lumber & Hardware, looking down Main](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Skybergs_Hardware_%26_Liquor_Store.jpg); [Main Street seen through the Wildwood window](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Main_Street_Willamina_from_the_Wildwood_Hotel.jpg); [1961 business district, Salem Public Library](http://photos.salemhistory.net/u?%2Fmax%2C3679=); current aerial of the downtown strip and the treed watercourse on its south side.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: building a long two-storey brick Main Street; using 2009 or 1961 shop names as if current; omitting the tree-lined creek/river immediately south of the shops. OSM already has many Willamina footprints (PLAN.md: 1,006 within the search radius), so TW-5 should upgrade a few key facades rather than invent the whole town.

### Historic two-storey brick commercial block (Antiques / Main Street)
- What / where: The principal historic commercial building on Main Street, a two-storey tan-brick row with several shop bays. In later photos the ground floor of one bay is lettered “ANTIQUES.” It sits in the tight downtown cluster (visible as the taller brick volume in the 2009 streetscape). Exact street number not independently confirmed. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Two storeys. The upper facade of the antiques bay has three window bays. Footprint and height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for two storeys and the brick form — source: [Commons, downtown Main Street, 2009](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Oregon_downtown_Main_Street.JPG); [Commons, Antiques and the Alley](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Antiques_and_the_Alley_on_Main_Street.jpg)
- Materials & colours: Tan / buff brick; maroon or dark reddish-brown window frames; a decorative brick cornice. In the 2009 view, the left half of the upper floor is painted cream or white while the right half is bare tan brick with slightly arched or pedimented window heads. Ground-floor shopfronts have been modernised (a brown band with “ANTIQUES” in white in the later close-up; dark awnings and hanging baskets in 2009). The side wall of one bay is painted white; a side alley has a brown metal gate. — confidence: high for those dated photos — source: same
- Distinctive features: This is the building that reads as “old Main Street.” The three upper windows, maroon frames, and brick cornice are the cues.
- Photos: the two Commons files above, and the slightly out-of-focus view of this block from inside the Wildwood Hotel.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: painting the whole upper floor one colour, or flattening the cornice. Shop names will change; keep the brick form.

### Wildwood Hotel (originally the Commercial Hotel)
- What / where: 150 NE Main Street, in the downtown core. Built near the end of the 19th century as the Commercial Hotel for loggers and brick-plant workers. Originally three-and-a-half storeys; reduced to two storeys after a fire in the 1930s. Renamed Wildwood after a 2003 purchase (later ownership changes are recorded in the press; use the building, not the operator). World x,z: unknown. Nominatim did not return a reliable point for this address (it snapped to the Main Street centreline in Polk County); place it in the Main Street commercial cluster from aerial / OSM, not at 45.067°N.
- Size: Two storeys now (was 3½ before the 1930s fire). Six small hotel rooms upstairs; restaurant and bar on the ground floor (2009 and 2020 descriptions). Footprint and height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for two storeys and the late-19th-century origin; medium for the current interior layout still matching those descriptions — source: [Smoke Signals, “Tribal member purchases Willamina hotel and restaurant,” 30 December 2020](https://www.smokesignals.org/articles/2020/12/30/tribal-member-purchases-willamina-hotel-and-restaurant/); [The Oregonian, 11 January 2009](https://www.oregonlive.com/travel/2009/01/oregon_offtrack_willamina.html)
- Materials & colours: Light-coloured vertical metal or board siding on the upper wall; a cream hanging sign with dark serif letters “WILDWOOD HOTEL” and a smaller “RESTAURANT” banner; a brown / bronze standing-seam metal awning with string lights; gray or taupe lower wall; wood-framed glazed doors lettered “HOTEL” and “CAFE”; a wooden porch or deck with a railing. A neighbouring wall of exposed red brick and a bright turquoise painted wooden building with outdoor seating appear beside it in the hotel photograph. The 2009 Oregonian description of the hotel at night as an “amber lantern in the shadows” is atmospheric, not a materials specification. — confidence: high for the dated exterior photo; medium for current paint of the neighbours — source: [Commons, Wildwood Hotel and Restaurant](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wildwood_Hotel_and_Restaurant.jpg)
- Distinctive features: The hanging hotel sign and metal awning are the Main Street landmark. It is the character building of downtown.
- Photos: [hotel sign, awning and entrance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wildwood_Hotel_and_Restaurant.jpg); [Main Street seen from a Wildwood window](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Main_Street_Willamina_from_the_Wildwood_Hotel.jpg); [interior music photo, 2015, not useful for the facade](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Live_Music_at_the_Wildwood_Hotel.jpg).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: modelling it as the original 3½-storey hotel, or as a generic two-storey box without the hanging sign and metal awning. Do not use the 19th-century height.

### Hampton Lumber mill (Willamina mill area)
- What / where: Hampton Lumber’s Willamina sawmill, official address 1000 Willamina Creek Road, north of downtown along Willamina Creek. The city’s history page places the demolished Pacific Face Brick plant “north of downtown near today’s Hampton Lumber Mills.” This was Hampton’s first mill (operations in the community since 1942). A 1946 fire destroyed the then Willamina Lumber Company structures; the mill was rebuilt and has been renovated since, including a modernisation described on the company page as underway to keep the mill competitive. World x,z: unknown. Nominatim did not return this address; place the complex from aerial imagery on the creek north of the residential grid (the mill occupies a bend of the creek, with forest on the east and fields on the west, and housing immediately to the southeast).
- Size: A large industrial complex stretching along the creek road: a long, hangar-like white-roofed mill building with lumber stacks along both long sides; a second cluster of white-roofed buildings and large lumber-storage yards to the southwest; access roads and log/lumber decks. Exact length, width and building heights in metres: unknown (measure from a georeferenced aerial; do not invent). The company states an annual production of 500 million board feet of Douglas-fir; that is a production figure, not a building size. — confidence: high that this is a large, multi-building mill with extensive yards; low for metric dimensions — source: [Hampton Lumber, Willamina](https://www.hamptonlumber.com/willamina/); [City of Willamina, History](https://www.willaminaoregon.gov/history/); current ESRI World Imagery of the mill (fetched 13 November 2026)
- Materials & colours: Long industrial sheds with light / white roofs (aerial). Yards of raw lumber (orange-brown packs) and white-wrapped packaged units. A hillside evening photograph looks down onto stacked lumber, yellow mill equipment and a long low industrial building. An older Yamhill County scenic photograph labelled “Willamina Lumber Company” shows a blue-gray metal industrial building with a tall stack, green/gray open sheds, and fresh-cut lumber behind a chain-link fence — treat that image as historic or undated, not as proof of the current paint scheme (the mill has been rebuilt and renovated). — confidence: high for the current aerial massing (white roofs, lumber yards along the creek); low for wall colours of specific sheds — source: [Commons, Willamina in the Evening](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_in_the_Evening_.jpg); [Commons, Willamina Lumber Company scenic image yamDA0104](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Lumber_Company_(Yamhill_County,_Oregon_scenic_images)_(yamDA0104).jpg); current aerial
- Distinctive features: The biggest built form in Willamina, obvious from the hills and from the air. Rail access is part of the site’s history and the company still lists train among shipping methods. Log trucks and lumber stacks are the ground-level cues. The creek’s tree belt wraps the east side of the mill.
- Photos: current aerial of the creek-side complex; [evening view over the lumber yard](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_in_the_Evening_.jpg); [older scenic ground view, use with caution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Lumber_Company_(Yamhill_County,_Oregon_scenic_images)_(yamDA0104).jpg); historic brick-plant photos (for what is *gone*) on the [Oregon Encyclopedia, Willamina](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/willamina_city_/).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: modelling the 1907–1976 Pacific Face Brick plant (razed 1976, nothing remains); using a 1940s or undated “Willamina Lumber Company” photo as the current mill; under-sizing the yards. A low-poly mill is still the landmark that makes Willamina a timber town from the jet or the highway approach. The company page notes an ongoing renovation — a current aerial should be the footprint source.

### Pacific Face Brick Company site (gone — do not model as standing)
- What / where: A brick plant established in 1907 by O.K. Edwards, north of downtown near today’s Hampton mill. It supplied cream-coloured and other bricks used around the Willamette Valley (Portland Art Museum, Jackson Tower, Lloyd Center, and the Yamhill County Courthouse are cited in secondary sources). Closed in 1974; buildings destroyed in 1976. World x,z: not applicable as a standing building.
- Size: Historic production figures (1 to 4.5 million bricks a year; 350 to 400 tons of clay per year to Pacific Stoneware) are not building dimensions. Footprint: unknown, and irrelevant if the buildings are gone. — confidence: high that nothing remains — source: [City of Willamina, History](https://www.willaminaoregon.gov/history/); [Oregon Encyclopedia, Willamina (city)](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/willamina_city_/)
- Materials & colours: Historic plant photos exist (Oregon Historical Society images on the encyclopedia page). Local clay was light in colour; red clay was brought from Newberg and buff clay from Buena Vista.
- Distinctive features: Historically important, but the site is not a current landmark. The nickname “The Little Town with the Big Payroll” and “Timbertown, U.S.A.” come from this era and from later mills.
- Photos: [encyclopedia historic plant images](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/willamina_city_/).
- Notes for the game: Do not add brick-plant kilns or stacks as if they still stand.

### Willamina Historical Museum (first church, 1887)
- What / where: 188 NE D Street (city and Travel Oregon also give 188 D Street / 188 NE D Street), a short walk off Main. Nominatim: 45.078673°N, 123.482869°W, typed as a museum. The city’s museum page says it is in one of the oldest buildings in the city, originally the first church, built in 1887. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Built 1887 as a church. Storeys, footprint and height in metres: unknown. No sourced exterior description was found. — confidence: high for the address, 1887 origin, and current museum use; low for appearance — source: [City of Willamina, Museum](https://www.willaminaoregon.gov/departments/museum/); [Travel Oregon, Willamina Historical Museum](https://traveloregon.com/things-to-do/oregon-attractions/museums/willamina-historical-museum/)
- Materials & colours: Unknown from a labelled, dated exterior photo. Do not invent a white steeple or assume it looks like the later red-brick United Methodist church on Main (see below).
- Distinctive features: An 1887 wood church converted to a museum; interior collections include scale models of a logging mill, the brick factory and a 1900s downtown, which are not exterior cues.
- Photos: none found that clearly show this building’s current facade. The 2009 Main-at-D photograph may include nearby buildings but is not labelled as the museum.
- Notes for the game: Owner photos are needed before modelling this. Easy to get wrong: using the later red-brick Methodist church as if it were the 1887 museum.

### Willamina City Hall
- What / where: 411 NE C Street (city and county directory). Photographed 21 March 2018 at 45.079769°N, 123.485044°W. A house-like civic building, not a downtown storefront. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: One-and-a-half storeys (steep gables with an upper floor in the roof). Footprint and height in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for the form and address — source: [City of Willamina, Administration / City Hall](https://www.willaminaoregon.gov/departments/administration/); [Commons, Willamina City Hall, 21 March 2018](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_City_Hall.jpg)
- Materials & colours: White horizontal lap siding, dark gray asphalt-shingle roof, white trim, black shutters on some windows. A steep side-gable roof with two street-facing gable ends (a house or Cape-like plan). The right-hand gable has tall, narrow windows (some with rounded heads) and rectangular windows. A recessed entry sits under the left gable. Front lawn, a low stone retaining wall, a flagpole, and a black-and-white sign lettered “WILLAMINA CITY HALL.” An earlier Commons photo shows a wooden version of the same sign. — confidence: high for 2018 — source: [2018 photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_City_Hall.jpg); [earlier wide view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Willamina_Oregon_city_hall.JPG)
- Distinctive features: The sign and the white double-gable house form are the civic cue. It looks like a converted house, not a brick town hall.
- Photos: the two Commons files above (the 2018 image is also used as the Wikipedia infobox photograph).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a brick or modernist civic block, or placing city hall on Main Street.

### Dr. Andrew Kershaw House (National Register)
- What / where: 472 E Main Street, on the eastern stretch of Main, more residential than the commercial core. Built 1907; Colonial Revival; listed on the National Register (NRIS 89000122). World x,z: unknown. Nominatim did not return a reliable point for this address; use 472 E Main on the east end of Main Street, not the false centreline snap described above.
- Size: Two-storey hip-roofed main block plus a one-and-a-half-storey gable-roofed ell (nomination). Height and footprint in metres: unknown. — confidence: high for storeys, date and style — source: [NPS / NRHP nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/a4a16115-2169-4515-a72e-4c396647e938); [NPGallery asset 89000122](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/89000122)
- Materials & colours: Shiplap siding, now pale gray / white, with gray trim and a gray-shingled hip roof; a brick chimney; two hip dormers with decorative swags; a full-width one-storey front porch with classical columns and a white railing; a second-storey balcony with a railing across the front; a central oval (oxeye) window with a decorative frame and keystones. The Commons photograph shows this composition clearly, including the address number 472 on a porch post. — confidence: high — source: nomination; [Commons, Dr. Andrew Kershaw House](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Andrew_Kershaw_House_Willamina_Oregon.JPG)
- Distinctive features: The oval window, hip dormers and full porch are the cues. It is the town’s NRHP house, but it sits on the edge of Main rather than in the shop row, so a player may pass it without noticing unless the oval window is modelled.
- Photos: [Commons facade](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Andrew_Kershaw_House_Willamina_Oregon.JPG).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a plain two-storey box, or placing it in the tight downtown brick row.

## Which matter most
Ranked by how much a player in a car, on a bike, or in the jet would notice them (not by historic importance alone):

1. **Hampton Lumber mill** — the largest built form in town: long white-roofed mill buildings and lumber yards along the creek north of downtown, obvious from the hills, the air, and the north approach.
2. **Wildwood Hotel** — the character building on Main Street: two storeys, hanging hotel sign, metal awning, next to the historic brick block.
3. **Main Street two-storey tan-brick commercial block** — the one stretch that reads as “old downtown” among otherwise plain one-storey shops.
4. **Willamina City Hall** — a white, house-like double-gable building with a prominent “WILLAMINA CITY HALL” sign; the civic cue just off Main.
5. **Willamina High School / elementary campus** — a large low orange-brick campus northeast of downtown, the biggest building group after the mill.

A confirmed water tower, or the 1887 museum once it is photographed, could displace City Hall or the school on a later pass.

## Gaps — what you couldn't find, and what to ask the owner
- **Exterior photo and a short description of the 1887 church / Willamina Historical Museum** at 188 NE D Street.
- **Willamina water tower**: location, height, colour and a current photo. The city has a “Water Tower View” page, but no usable image or dimensions were retrieved.
- **Current street-level photos of the Hampton mill** (the company notes a renovation; an owner drive-by or a dated 2024–2026 aerial should be the footprint source). Metric mill-building sizes were not published.
- **World x,z** for every site. These notes give WGS84 where Nominatim or photo GPS was reliable, plus the public minimap town label (x=-3640, z=3330). Conversion needs `data/raw/naip_info.json`.
- **Metric heights** for Main Street buildings, City Hall, Kershaw House, the fire station and the school. Lidar already in the tile pipeline may be the best source for collision tops.
- **Whether Skybergs, the antiques shop, and the 2009 bar / market still occupy the same buildings.** Model forms first; lettering only with a current photo.
- **Galloping Goose train car** mentioned in the 2009 Oregonian article: still in town, moved, or gone?
- **Oregon Historic Sites records** for downtown Willamina beyond the Kershaw House were not pulled as a full survey. A city or SHPO reconnaissance survey, if one exists, would help.
- **Exact official address of the West Valley Fire District station.**
