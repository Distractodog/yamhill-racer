# Town landmarks (McMinnville) — research notes (13 November 2026)
Status: draft

## Summary (≤ 10 lines: what the coding agent needs to know)
McMinnville’s recognisable core is a tight, zero-setback commercial street wall on NE Third Street (National Register historic district, listed 14 September 1987). Most historic buildings are one or two storeys of brick or stuccoed brick; Hotel Oregon is the only four-storey historic building on the street. Fabric awnings are common at first-floor storefronts. Armstrong red maples form a canopy or “tunnel” over a 60 ft (18.29 m) right-of-way. East of town, off the Highway 18 bypass, the Evergreen Aviation & Space Museum campus is dominated by two huge hangar-like museums and a Boeing 747 mounted on the Wings & Waves Waterpark roof. The Aviation Building was built around the Hughes H-4 “Spruce Goose.” South of downtown, Linfield University’s historic core is Pioneer Hall (1883, red brick Greek cross with a cupola, in an oak grove) facing Melrose Hall (1928–29, three-storey brick with a four-column sandstone portico). No classic downtown elevated water tower was found; the city’s visible finished-water storage is four tanks on Fox Ridge, west of the city limits. World x,z coordinates are unknown (game origin is the Delphian campus; this notes pack has no projection constants). All dimensions below are converted to metres with the original unit given; unsourced sizes are marked unknown.

## Facts

### Third Street downtown historic district (streetscape)
- What / where: McMinnville’s main commercial street, running east–west. The 1987 National Register district is an irregular area of about 15.2 acres (6.15 ha) covering portions of 15 blocks, with a strong central focus along Third Street from Baker Street to Galloway Street; the eastern edge is the (then Southern Pacific, now Union Pacific) railroad tracks. The 2025 city streetscape plan describes the Third Street Improvement Project as nine blocks from a T-intersection at NW Adams Street (City Park / library end) east to NE Johnson Street, including an at-grade rail crossing at Hembree and the Highway 99W couplet at Adams and Baker. Coordinates for the district core are unknown as a single official point; Hotel Oregon at Third and Evans is 45.21019°N, 123.19422°W (OpenStreetMap Nominatim). World x,z: unknown.
- Size: In 1987 the district contained 66 buildings: 22 Primary Significant (1880–1912), 30 Secondary Significant (1913–1937), and 14 non-contributing, plus one vacant lot not tallied. A parsed reading of the nomination’s building descriptions found one four-storey building (Hotel Oregon), three three-storey buildings, and the great majority one or two storeys (counts are from those descriptions; a few entries do not state a storey count). Overall building heights in metres: unknown (no sourced eaves or ridge heights). Existing Third Street right-of-way averages 60 ft (18.29 m) building-face to building-face: 10 ft (3.05 m) sidewalk + 8 ft (2.44 m) parking + 12 ft (3.66 m) travel lane + 12 ft (3.66 m) travel lane + 8 ft (2.44 m) parking + 10 ft (3.05 m) sidewalk. — confidence: high for bounds, storey pattern, and the 60 ft section; medium for the exact 1987-to-now building count (the 2025 plan says “64 historic properties” downtown) — source: [1987 NRHP nomination (NPS text)](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/87001366_text); [2025 Third Street Streetscape Plan Report](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/third_street_improvement/page/24441/2025_0618_mac3rd_streetscape_plan_report_tooledesign.pdf)
- Materials & colours: The nomination states the “primary building materials on historic buildings are brick and stucco.” Other historic materials include cast stone, pressed metal, and wood ornament (cornices, pediments, friezes, door and window surrounds). Many first floors were later remodelled with tile, stucco, or marble; many storefronts have large single-pane aluminium-framed display windows; many transoms have been covered with plywood or stucco. Street: asphalt (paved 1912). Sidewalks and curbs: concrete; typical scoring is a 2 × 2 ft (0.61 × 0.61 m) pattern, with some later tiles or pavers. 1987 public furnishings: metal lamp posts at major intersections, wooden street furniture, trash receptacles, and mid-block curb “eyebrows.” Brick colour is not given as a single official value; named exceptions include “native Willamina clay brick” (Masonic Building) and “square red brick” (Jameson Hardware). Roofscape is mostly flat or very low parapets on the commercial blocks, with California Mission red clay-tile parapets on some late-1920s buildings at the east end. — confidence: high for materials and the 1912 paving date; medium for how many storefronts still look as they did in 1987 — source: [1987 NRHP nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/87001366_text); [2025 Streetscape Plan Report](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/third_street_improvement/page/24441/2025_0618_mac3rd_streetscape_plan_report_tooledesign.pdf)
- Distinctive features:
  - Continuous zero-setback street wall of narrow historic storefronts facing Third Street; most buildings occupy the full lot frontage.
  - Fabric awnings (and some painted metal marquees) at the top of many first-floor storefronts; the later Hotel Oregon inventory notes a “handsome awning” added in a renovation. Do not assume every bay has an awning; the nomination documents them building by building.
  - Street trees: the 1987 nomination already recorded “a variety of now mature street trees” in the district. The 2025 city plan says Third Street’s trees, “primarily Armstrong red maples,” are a signature of downtown and create an “arch” or “tunnel” effect over the roadway. First street trees and mid-block curb extensions date to the 1976 reconstruction (city 2025 plan). The American Planning Association’s 2015 Great Places citation also credits huge trees selected and planted in a major early-2000s streetscape. Some trees are now in compromised health and their roots have lifted sidewalks; a 2025 improvement project (including a possible curbless street) aims to preserve existing trees where feasible. Model the current maple canopy unless the owner says the rebuild has already changed it.
  - Historic names along Third and the immediately adjoining streets (addresses use the current NE prefix; the 1987 nomination often says “East Third”). See the building entries below and the compact list at the end of this block.
  - Styles represented in the district (nomination): Italianate, Queen Anne, Richardsonian Romanesque, Commercial, American Renaissance, Craftsman, California Mission, and Modernistic.

### Hotel Oregon (historic name: Hotel Elberton)
- What / where: Corner of NE Third Street and NE Evans Street, mid-downtown. Current common name McMenamins Hotel Oregon. City historic page: 310 NE Evans Street; inventory and nomination also use 503–513 East / NE Third Street (same corner lot). OSM: 45.21019°N, 123.19422°W. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Four storeys; tax lot 8,078 sq ft (750.5 m²) — this is the lot, not a measured building footprint. Building length × width and height in metres: unknown. Built 1905 as a two-storey hotel, planned as four storeys; the third and fourth floors were added in 1910 (city historic page) or circa 1912 (city inventory). McMenamins bought and restored the hotel in 1998, finishing the fourth floor, and reopened in 1999; a rooftop bar/restaurant sits on top. The city calls it Yamhill County’s largest historic hotel. — confidence: high for four storeys, 1905 start date, and the two-then-four-storey history; medium for the 1910 vs c. 1912 addition date; low/unknown for metric height — source: [City of McMinnville, Hotel Oregon / McMenamins](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/cd/page/hotel-oregon-mcmenamins); [City historic inventory A834](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a834_inventory.pdf); [1987 NRHP nomination, property 24](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/87001366_text)
- Materials & colours: “Native stone and brick,” Richardsonian Romanesque (city page). Inventory: cubical brick four-storey structure; façade six bays; one-over-one double-hung windows predominate, with some small multi-light casements; windows inset with flat arches and stone sills; corbelled brick belt courses at the third and fourth floors; a rock-faced stone belt course between the first and second floors; inset panels of aggregate river rock; corbelled brick cornice and a panelled parapet. West elevation eight bays; rear “unadorned brick,” symmetrical, seven bays, with a central raised chimney and arched window openings. Storefronts have been remodelled; a later renovation added a fabric awning and uniform sign plaques. — confidence: high — source: [inventory A834](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a834_inventory.pdf); [city historic page](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/cd/page/hotel-oregon-mcmenamins)
- Distinctive features: Corner siting on Third and Evans, taller than the neighbouring two-storey street wall; an arched balcony opening from the second floor just right of centre on the Third Street façade; a later neon “Hotel Oregon” sign (city page); rooftop bar (visible as a fifth-level terrace, but the historic massing is four storeys — do not invent a fifth masonry floor); Otis passenger elevator (historic, not a visible exterior cue).
- Photos:
  - [Wikimedia: Hotel Oregon McMinnville Oregon.JPG](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hotel_Oregon_McMinnville_Oregon.JPG) — exterior of the hotel.
  - [Category: Hotel Oregon](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Hotel_Oregon)
  - [City page photo](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/styles/full_node_primary/public/imageattachments/planning/page/23376/22_mcmenamins_social1080x1080.png) — recent view of the building.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: leaving it the same height as the rest of Third Street; using only brick and omitting the stone belt and river-rock panels; placing it mid-block instead of on the Evans corner; inventing a pointed tower or a fifth masonry storey for the rooftop bar.

### Masonic Building

### Evergreen Aviation & Space Museum campus (overview)
- What / where: 500 NE Captain Michael King Smith Way, McMinnville, OR 97128, east of downtown, entered from the Highway 18 bypass / Three Mile Lane corridor. OSM way centroid: 45.20379°N, 123.14292°W (bounding box approx. 45.20176–45.20595°N, 123.14656–123.13932°W). World x,z: unknown. The operating organisation’s current site is [noramaerospace.org](https://noramaerospace.org/) (North American Aerospace Museum / NORAM); the homepage still displays the name Evergreen Aviation & Space Museum, which is also the name used in the game brief, the 2024 NRHP nomination, and OSM.
- Size: A campus of large, separate buildings around a central parking plaza, not one shed. The 2024 NRHP nomination for the Hughes Flying Boat describes the approach and layout (see next entries for per-building sizes). — confidence: high for address, OSM coordinates, and the campus arrangement — source: [NORAM / museum site](https://noramaerospace.org/); [2024 NRHP nomination, Hughes Flying Boat](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=NR_Noms%2FRS100009992.pdf); [OSM Nominatim]
- Materials & colours: See the individual hangars. Campus-wide, the aviation and space buildings are matching hangar-like halls in a similar language (architects: Ankrom Moisan for the Aviation Building; Scott Edwards Architecture for later campus buildings).
- Distinctive features: From Highway 18, a road “designed as a runway” approaches the Aviation Building and, in the nomination’s words, “appears at first to dead-end at the nose of the H-4 Hercules,” then turns right into the parking area. From that plaza: Space Building to the east (completed 2008), Theater Building to the south (completed 2007), Aviation Building to the west (completed 2001). The Wings & Waves Waterpark, with a 747 on the roof, is a further building on the same campus.
- Photos: [Wikimedia category: Evergreen Aviation & Space Museum](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Evergreen_Aviation_%26_Space_Museum); [Hoffman Construction, Aviation Museum project photo](https://www.hoffmancorp.com/project/evergreen-aviation-museum/).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a single hangar; putting the 747 on the Spruce Goose building; omitting the runway-like entry road aimed at the H-4’s nose (that is the first thing a driver on Hwy 18 would notice). These buildings are far larger than anything downtown.

### Aviation Building (Spruce Goose hangar)
- What / where: West side of the museum plaza; built specifically to house the H-4. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: 121,000 sq ft (11,241 m²), “ten-story-tall,” completed 2001 (2024 NRHP nomination). The original contractor, Hoffman Construction, published 120,000 SF and a peak height of 130 ft (39.62 m), with five 300 ft (91.44 m) scissors trusses. Architect Mitch Darby (Ankrom Moisan) also published 120,000 s.f. Scott Edwards Architecture’s later campus page says 125,000 sf for the Aviation Museum. Use 121,000 sq ft and “ten-storey” from the 2024 nomination as the most recent official figure; treat 130 ft (39.62 m) as a contractor figure for peak height (high/medium confidence); do not invent a single reconciled number. — confidence: high that it is a ~11,000+ m², very tall, 2001 hangar; medium for the exact peak height and which square-footage figure is measured floor area — source: [2024 NRHP nomination](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=NR_Noms%2FRS100009992.pdf); [Hoffman Construction](https://www.hoffmancorp.com/project/evergreen-aviation-museum/); [Mitch Darby Architect](https://mitchdarbyarchitect.com/evergreen-aviation-mcminnville-oregon); [Scott Edwards Architecture](https://www.seallp.com/work/hospitality/evergreen-museum)
- Materials & colours: South curtain wall of glass windows; three hangar doors on the north side; skylight windows at the centre of the towering roof; walls and floor are concrete (NRHP). Hoffman: site-cast precast wall panels with seeded 2-inch (51 mm) aggregate, stacked four high. Structural steel scissors trusses. — confidence: high — source: same
- Distinctive features: The H-4 sits in a concrete pit designed for its graving-dock cradle, so the base of the fuselage is at floor level; the aircraft “occupies an acre-and-a-half” (about 0.61 ha) of the floor (NRHP). Other historic aircraft are displayed around it. The building was finished around the reassembled seaplane.
- Photos: [Hoffman project page](https://www.hoffmancorp.com/project/evergreen-aviation-museum/); [Darby: exterior and skylight structure](https://mitchdarbyarchitect.com/evergreen-aviation-mcminnville-oregon); Wikimedia category above.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a generic quonset or a too-short hangar (this roof has to clear a tail about 24 m high plus hanging aircraft); opaque south wall (it is a glass curtain); putting vehicle access through the glass south wall rather than the north hangar doors; sitting the H-4 on top of a floor slab instead of in a pit with the hull at floor level.

### Hughes H-4 Hercules (“Spruce Goose”)
- What / where: Inside the Aviation Building, 500 NE Captain Michael King Smith Way. Silver-grey / silver-aluminium painted wooden flying boat. World x,z: unknown (indoor exhibit, but the nose is composed to be visible from the entry road).
- Size (official museum page and 2024 NRHP nomination, original units first):
  - Wingspan: 319 ft 11 in (97.51 m). The nomination also says “320 feet tip to tip” in a later sentence; use 319 ft 11 in as the stated measurement.
  - Length (fuselage): 218 ft 8 in (66.65 m).
  - Height: museum page 79 ft 4 in (24.18 m); NRHP nomination 79 ft 3 3/8 in (24.17 m) “from the bottom of the hull to the top of the tail.” Report both; they differ by 5/8 in (16 mm).
  - Empty weight: 300,000 lb (136,078 kg).
  - Eight Pratt & Whitney R-4360 Wasp Major engines; each Hamilton Standard four-blade propeller is 17 ft 2 in (5.23 m) in diameter (NRHP). — confidence: high — source: [NORAM, The Spruce Goose](https://noramaerospace.org/exhibit/the-spruce-goose/); [2024 NRHP nomination](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=NR_Noms%2FRS100009992.pdf)
- Materials & colours: Airframe is Duramold birch wood lamination (museum: almost the whole aircraft is birch, despite the nickname; NRHP: 94 percent yellow birch, plus small amounts of other woods), with a smooth surface coated in silver-aluminium paint; metal engines, fittings, and four-blade metal propellers; fabric-covered control surfaces except the flaps; a pontoon under the outer third of each wing. — confidence: high — source: same
- Distinctive features: High-wing, eight-engine, single-hull flying boat, vastly larger than the hangar’s other aircraft; tail described in the nomination as towering to “nearly eight-stories.”
- Photos: [NORAM exhibit page photo](https://noramaerospace.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2B3A1028.jpg); Wikimedia category for the museum.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: painting it brown “spruce”; shrinking it to “large airliner” scale (the wingspan is almost the length of the 300 ft trusses); mounting it on a stand above the floor rather than hull-at-floor in a pit.

### Wings & Waves Waterpark and rooftop Boeing 747
- What / where: On the Evergreen museum campus; a separate building with a complete 747 mounted on the roof, used as the starting point for water slides. World x,z: unknown (on campus, near the museums; exact offset not sourced).
- Size: Waterpark building: 71,000 sq ft (6,596 m²) (Counsilman-Hunsaker / *American Builders Quarterly*, 2012). Building height and the height of the 747 above grade are not agreed: Hoffman Construction says the 328,000 lb (148,778 kg) jetliner was lifted “65 feet in the air” (19.81 m); the aquatics article says the 747 “sits 62 feet in the air” (18.90 m); *Aviation Week* called it a 60 ft (18.29 m) high building. Do not pick one number; model a roughly 18–20 m building with a 747 on the roof and flag the conflict. Aircraft identity: Boeing 747-132 (SF) N481EV, manufacturer serial 19896, formerly Delta Air Lines’ first 747, later Evergreen International (World Airline News, 3 September 2011; Wikimedia file title). ### Linfield University main quad (campus core)
- What / where: Linfield University McMinnville campus, official address 900 SE Baker Street, McMinnville, OR 97128 (university site). The city’s historic inventories use 900 SW Baker Street for Pioneer Hall and Melrose Hall (Baker Street changes designation; prefer the university’s SE Baker address for the campus, and site the historic buildings from descriptions and a map, not from the prefix alone). OSM campus centroid: 45.19847°N, 123.19814°W (bounding box approx. 45.19421–45.20324°N, 123.20426–123.19456°W), immediately south of downtown, across Cozine Creek. World x,z: unknown. The university says the campus has more than 60 buildings on 189 acres (76.5 ha).
- Size: The historic core is not the whole 189 acres. Pioneer Hall stands on the south bank of Cozine Creek in an oak grove of about 3 acres (1.21 ha) and faces north toward the creek (1978 NRHP nomination). Melrose Hall faces north on Campus Drive (city inventory). The university describes “the academic quad” as eight buildings overlooking common grounds; the scenic Oak Grove in front of Pioneer Hall is the commencement lawn. A 1928–29 campus plan by Samuel Lancaster added “gracefully curved concrete drives and walks” and lawns (summarised from the campus-grounds inventory; the proposed Cozine lake was never built). — confidence: high for campus location, 189 acres, Pioneer’s grove and orientation, and Melrose’s Campus Drive frontage; medium for exactly which eight buildings formally count as “the academic quad” — source: [Linfield, About Our Campus](https://linfield.edu/conference-event-services/our-campus.html); [Linfield campus map (PDF)](https://www.linfield.edu/assets/files/admission/Linfield-Campus-Map.pdf); [Pioneer Hall NRHP nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/073b4ebb-b690-45c9-80c3-b2e4204470ff); [City inventory A503.1, campus grounds](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a503.1_inventory.pdf)
- Materials & colours: Historic core = red/brick academic buildings in an oak grove, with concrete walks and lawns; see Pioneer and Melrose. Later campus buildings vary and were not fully researched here.
- Distinctive features: Oak Grove (large Oregon white oaks; the “Old Oak” is a campus symbol — Oregon Encyclopedia). Cozine Creek on the north side of Pioneer, historically crossed by wooden bridges. Memorial Fountain is mapped on the official campus map (feature 34) but its dimensions were not sourced. A small Observatory (1894) stands just behind Pioneer Hall (campus-grounds inventory).
- Photos: [Linfield campus page](https://linfield.edu/conference-event-services/our-campus.html); [Oregon Encyclopedia, Melrose Hall 1929](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/media/uploads/Linfield_1929_Melrose_Hall_005521_sm.jpg); [Wikimedia: Category:Pioneer Hall (Oregon)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Pioneer_Hall_(Oregon)).

### Pioneer Hall (Linfield University)
- What / where: The original college building on the new 1881 campus, south bank of Cozine Creek, in the oak grove, facing north. Cornerstone 31 May 1882; completed March 1883 and dedicated 12 June 1883. Named Pioneer Hall in 1928 after Melrose Hall was completed. Listed on the National Register in 1978. City historic page: “Campus Drive | Built 1883.” World x,z: unknown.
- Size: A three-storey brick building on a high basalt basement (four storeys including the basement) plus a square central cupola / lantern. The 1885 college catalog, cited in the NRHP nomination, measured the building 106 ft by 79 ft (32.31 × 24.08 m). Overall height including cupola, in metres: unknown. The nomination, writing in 1976, called it still the tallest building in Yamhill County — that is a 1976 statement, not a current one (the Aviation Building is much taller). — confidence: high for the cross plan, storey arrangement, 1883 date, and the 1885 106 × 79 ft figure; unknown for current total height — source: [Pioneer Hall NRHP nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/073b4ebb-b690-45c9-80c3-b2e4204470ff); [City inventory A554](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a554_inventory.pdf); [City historic page](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/cd/page/pioneer-hall-linfield-university)
- Materials & colours: Italianate / “Bracketed Villa.” Red brick (the city page says the founder’s Ford Street plant produced 300,000 red bricks for it). High basement of roughly dressed local basalt, with a sandstone band at the top of the base and a brick belt course at the main floor. Strip pilasters. Low truncated hip roof with boxed eaves and decorative wood brackets; originally wood-shingled cupola roof with a shingled central spire (nomination). Regular one-over-one double-hung windows; arch heads and hoods change by floor — basement flat-arched, then first/second/third storeys with round, segmental, and flat heads respectively, each with bracketed hoods of the same shape. East and west elevations have small centred pediments (triangular); the north entry has a segmental pediment. Original four corbelled chimneys were removed in 1946. North entry: originally an exposed stair to a porch and two massive wood doors with a recessed fanlight; doors and some porch ornaments have been altered (inventory notes later replicated porch ornament and eight-foot (2.44 m) oak entry doors as a restoration item — confirm against a current photo). A single-storey chapel/stage wing telescopes from the south arm of the cross. The cupola is square, at the junction of the four wings, with three bays of louvered panels on each side; the city page describes a “white cupola.” The 1978 nomination: the “building’s white cupola is a visible landmark across the farm fields of Yamhill County.” — confidence: high for form, brick/basalt, window rhythm, and the white cupola; medium for how much 1946/later restoration is visible now — source: same
- Distinctive features: Truncated Greek-cross plan, tall white cupola, oak grove, north-facing entry toward Cozine Creek. This is the building a player should recognise as “the old college.”
- Photos:
  - [City page photo](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/styles/full_node_primary/public/imageattachments/planning/page/23373/22_linfieldpioneer_social1080x1080.png)
  - [Wikimedia: Category:Pioneer Hall (Oregon)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Pioneer_Hall_(Oregon))
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: a plain rectangular brick box; omitting the cross plan, the high dark basalt base, the changing window-arch shapes by floor, or the white cupola (the long-range cue); facing it the wrong way (it faces north, toward the creek and downtown, not toward Highway 99W).

### Melrose Hall
- What / where: Linfield’s main administration building, facing north on Campus Drive; completed 1929 (inventory: “1929 (1928 Sanborn)”). Architect: A.E. Doyle & Associates (Pietro Belluschi). Pioneer Hall was renamed when this building was completed. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Three-storey T-shaped brick mass with a hip roof (city inventory). Length × width and height in metres: unknown. The rear wing of the T holds a small auditorium of about 250–300 seats (now Richard & Lucille Ice Auditorium on the university map). — confidence: high for three storeys, T-plan, 1928–29, and the portico description; unknown for metric size — source: [City inventory A548](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a548_inventory.pdf); [Linfield campus map](https://www.linfield.edu/assets/files/admission/Linfield-Campus-Map.pdf)
- Materials & colours: Brick, with classical stone detailing. Symmetrical entry façade: a pedimented portico on four two-and-a-half-storey unfluted sandstone Ionic columns; Tuscan-Doric-derived pilasters behind them; college seal in light relief in the pediment; dentilled cast-stone cornice with a plain frieze; cast-stone belt course at first-floor level. Central bay: recessed high arched door with a cast-stone surround and console keystone; paired doors (six lights over recessed panels); eight-light transom and a half-round light behind a cast-iron screen; a tall 12/12/12 triple-hung stairwell window above; 12/12 double-hung windows flanking the entry. Each side of the front mass continues in seven bays, divided in a 2-3-2 rhythm by vertical columns of projecting brick rectangles, with brick quoins at the corners. First-floor windows: recessed herringbone brick arches over 12/12 sash; all other windows 8/12 double-hung. End bays have rectangular recessed doors with transoms and tall arched windows above. The rear wing continues the high arched window motif. — confidence: high (inventory is a primary architectural description) — source: [City inventory A548](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a548_inventory.pdf)
- Distinctive features: The four sandstone Ionic columns and pedimented portico are the recognisable face of the later campus, looking toward Pioneer Hall and the oak grove. It is classical and horizontal, in contrast to Pioneer’s vertical Italianate cupola.
- Photos: [Oregon Historical Society photo, Melrose Hall, 1929, via Oregon Encyclopedia](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/media/uploads/Linfield_1929_Melrose_Hall_005521_sm.jpg) (historic view; check a current photo for later changes). Inventory includes November 2001 and 1983 survey photos (in the city PDF).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: giving Melrose a cupola like Pioneer; omitting the four-column portico; using the same window rhythm on every floor (the herringbone-arched 12/12 first-floor windows are distinctive).

### Water storage — Fox Ridge tanks (and the missing “water tower”)
- What / where: The brief asks for “the water tower.” No downtown or highway-side elevated water tower (a tank on legs or a standpipe) was found in official city or utility pages, the 1987 historic-district nomination, the 2026 walking tour, Wikimedia’s McMinnville/Oregon water-tower categories, or OpenStreetMap Nominatim searches for “water tower McMinnville Oregon.” What does exist, and is the city’s finished-water storage, is four above-ground distribution reservoirs (tanks) on Fox Ridge Road, just west of the McMinnville city limits, at 12300 SW Fox Ridge Road (McMinnville Water & Light RFP). Water reaches them by gravity from the Norm Scott Water Treatment Plant through about 10 miles (16 km) of transmission pipe. World x,z: unknown; exact WGS84 coordinates were not confirmed from a primary map (Nominatim did not return the address in this pass).
- Size: Combined volume of the four tanks is 22.7 million gallons (85,900 m³). Individual tank diameters, heights, and appearance (ground-level cylinders vs elevated bowls): unknown. — confidence: high for “four tanks, Fox Ridge Road, just west of the city limits, 22.7 million gallons combined”; unknown for height and form — source: [McMinnville Water & Light, Water System/Watershed](https://www.mc-power.com/water/water-system-watershed/); [MW&L RFP, Fox Ridge Water Reservoir property](https://www.mc-power.com/bids/rfp-ground-lease-of-space-for-cellular-antenna-system/)
- Materials & colours: Unknown (typical modern steel or concrete reservoir tanks; do not invent colour or a “McMinnville” logo).
- Distinctive features: On a ridge west of town, these would be visible mainly from the air or from the western approaches, not as a downtown landmark. A 1908 plan for City Park included a small irrigation “water tower” for park sprinkling; no evidence was found that a historic park tower survives as a recognisable structure.
- Photos: none sourced that clearly show all four tanks close-up. Do not use an unsourced generic water-tower image.
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: inventing a classic elevated “cities” water tower in downtown McMinnville. Ask the owner which object they meant (Fox Ridge tanks, a feature visible on the satellite/lidar, or something else). If they mean Fox Ridge, get a current aerial or a site photo before modelling; heights are unknown.

### McMinnville Public Library (Carnegie) and City Park
- What / where: Library at 225 NW Adams Street, southeast corner of City Park, at the west end of Third Street (City historic page). Built 1913 (Carnegie grant 1912). City Park: 140 NW Park Drive, established 1908, at the west end of Third Street. OSM places the library at 45.20986°N, 123.19971°W but labels the address “Northeast Adams Street”; use the city’s 225 NW Adams Street. World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Original building: a rectangular brick block with a low-pitched hipped roof (city page and inventory). Footprint and height in metres: unknown. A 14,800 sq ft (1,375 m²) expansion and breezeway were added in 1982, “preserving the historic core” (city page). — confidence: high for address, 1913 date, and the 1982 addition area; unknown for original dimensions — source: [City historic page](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/cd/page/mcminnville-public-library); [City inventory A400](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a400_inventory.pdf); [City Park page](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/cd/page/city-park)
- Materials & colours: Inventory: regular rectangular block, low-pitched hipped roof; brick above the first floor, rough concrete below; wide eaves on paired brackets; arched double door on the north façade in concentric header arches; stained-glass lunette with fir trees, a lamp, and the words “Carnegie Library 1912.” Fenestration somewhat irregular: strips of narrow paired casements with transoms of small translucent lights continuous with the frieze; daylight basement 8/8 double-hung; frieze partly decorated with contrasting brick diamonds. Style in the walking tour: American Renaissance. — confidence: high — source: [inventory A400](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/archives/Historic_Resources/A_Book/a400_inventory.pdf)
- Distinctive features: Sits on the park edge, facing north, at the Adams Street / Third Street gateway to downtown. City Park includes the Soper Fountain (installed 1928, moved to the entry plaza in 1957) and the giant sequoia known as “Big Mac,” the city’s Christmas tree, near the library (city pages). The 1983 inventory also mentioned a monkey-puzzle tree; confirm whether it still stands before modelling it.
- Photos: [City library page photo](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/styles/full_node_primary/public/imageattachments/planning/page/23371/library.png); [City Park page](https://www.mcminnvilleoregon.gov/cd/page/city-park).
- Notes for the game: Easy to get wrong: hiding the Carnegie block behind the 1982 addition, or omitting the park / Big Mac sequoia at the west end of Third Street, which is how the downtown blocks terminate.

## Which matter most
Ranked by how much a player in a car, on a bike, or in the jet would notice them (not by historic importance alone):

1. **Boeing 747 on the Wings & Waves Waterpark roof** — a full jumbo jet on a building, with slides leaving the fuselage, is unique on the Highway 18 approach and from the air; nothing else in Yamhill County looks like this.
2. **Aviation Building (Spruce Goose hangar)** — a roughly 40 m-tall glass-and-concrete hangar, with a runway-like entry road aimed at the H-4’s nose, is the largest built form on the east side of town.
3. **Third Street downtown blocks (as one streetscape)** — the continuous one- and two-storey brick/stucco wall, fabric awnings, and Armstrong red maple tunnel are how a player knows they have arrived in McMinnville; this is also a default first TW-5 landmark.
4. **Hotel Oregon** — the only four-storey historic building on Third Street, on the Evans corner, with stone banding and a rooftop bar; it is the height spike that pokes out of the maple canopy.
5. **Pioneer Hall and the Linfield oak grove (with Melrose Hall’s portico as the facing piece)** — the 1883 red-brick Greek cross and white cupola in the oaks, facing Melrose’s four sandstone columns, reads as a college campus immediately south of downtown; the cupola was historically a landmark across the surrounding fields.

## Gaps — what you couldn't find, and what to ask the owner
- **Which “water tower”?** No classic elevated downtown or roadside water tower was found. Is the intended landmark the four Fox Ridge tanks west of town, a tank visible on the game’s satellite/lidar, or something else? If Fox Ridge, a current aerial or site photo and tank heights are needed.
- **World x,z** for every site (game origin is the Delphian campus). These notes give WGS84 only where OSM Nominatim provided a point.
- **Metric building heights** for downtown and Linfield (storeys are known; eaves/ridge heights are not). Lidar already used in the tile pipeline may be the best way to set collision tops, if the owner can share a height sample.
- **747 airframe dimensions** for N481EV specifically, and a single official figure for how high the waterpark roof / aircraft sit above grade (sources say 60, 62, or 65 ft).
- **Aviation Building peak height** is 130 ft only from the contractor; the 2024 nomination says “ten-story-tall” without metres. Square-footage figures also differ (120,000 / 121,000 / 125,000 sf).
- **Whether the 2025 Third Street Improvement Project** has already started changing kerbs, parking, or the maple canopy as of this research date.
- **Current photos** the owner may have from driving Third Street, the Hwy 18 museum approach, and the Linfield oak grove — especially useful to check awning colours, Hotel Oregon’s neon sign, Melrose today vs the 1929 photograph, and whether the Wright pediment lettering is still readable.
- **Yamhill County Courthouse** (present building): no primary architectural description found; do not invent one.
- **Exact academic-quad roster** of the “eight buildings” and Memorial Fountain dimensions.
- **SE vs SW Baker Street** when placing Pioneer and Melrose relative to the campus origin — confirm on satellite.
