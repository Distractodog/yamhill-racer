# Main Building — research notes (2026-11-13)
Status: draft

## Summary (≤ 10 lines: what the coding agent needs to know)
The Main Building of the Delphian School, 20950 SW Rock Creek Road, Sheridan, Oregon, is the 1933 Art Deco former St. Francis Xavier Novitiate (architects Poole & McGonigle; buff/orange brick veneer added in 1955). The front faces south-southeast. A four-storey central core is flanked by recessed three-storey wings; a tower about six storeys tall marks a recessed stone entrance with the lettering THE DELPHIAN SCHOOL, steps, and a patterned brick plaza — there is no projecting canopy. Front-core windows are single rectangular openings in vertical bays between brick piers, grouped 2+2 on each side of the tower, four storeys; the three-storey wings are an even grid (east wing about 12–14 bays). Coping is a thin light-coloured cap; spandrels are inset brick with light sills, not large stone panels. Behind the front range, a courtyard holds a pointed-arch brick chapel with a grey metal gable and stained glass, a one-storey kitchen/dining colonnade, a small stone fountain, curved cream seat-walls, and a black steel fire stair on a cream 1955 dorm wing. Primary modelling refs: the school's aerial and the 2018 entrance/courtyard 360s. World x,z: unknown. Height in metres: unknown. OpenStreetMap's footprint is simplified, incomplete, and sits about 5 m south-east of the real roofs — do not place the model from uncorrected OSM.

## Facts

### The building as a whole
- What / where: Main academic, dining, chapel and (in the wings) dormitory building of the Delphian School, 20950 SW Rock Creek Road, Sheridan, Oregon 97378. About 3.2 km (2 miles) north-west of Sheridan, west of Rock Creek Road, on a wooded hill the 1984 inventory describes as 244 m (800 feet; original unit feet) above the valley floor. The long axis runs east-north-east / west-south-west; the front faces south-south-east (facade heading about 152° from OSM edges). Approximate geographic centre 45.1076°N, 123.4479°W (OSM polygon centroid; Wikipedia's point 45.107282°N, 123.44801°W sits nearer the south front). World x,z: unknown.
- Size: Documented as a four-storey building (Oregon Historic Sites Database) with a “central four story core, flanked by recessed three story wings” and “a central tower [that] rises about six stories in height” (Yamhill County Cultural Resources Inventory, 1984). Overall roof mass on Esri World Imagery (zoom 19, 0.211 m/pixel at this latitude): about 116 m east–west × 89 m north–south bounding box; south frontage corner-to-corner along the facade about 123 m. Front-range depth about 12–16 m (a relatively shallow wing). Chapel gable roof about 27 × 18 m. North rear block (1955 dining/dorm roof) about 50 × 17 m. Height in metres: unknown (no surveyed figure found). — confidence: high on storey counts (inventory + photographs); medium on metre plan dimensions (satellite roof-edge measurements, ±1–2 m typical; see method under Sources); low/unknown on height in metres — source: Oregon Historic Sites Database record 19820; 1984 inventory PDF; Esri World Imagery mosaic and OSM way 678519248 (see Sources).
- Materials & colours: Concrete structure, now sheathed in buff/orange brick of varying shades (the 1955 remodelling). The 1984 inventory: “buff brick veneer and some slightly pink cast stone details.” Cast stone (cream to slightly pink) is concentrated on the tower's south face, the entrance surround, window sills, and a thin parapet coping. Roofs of the wings and core are nearly flat membranes, light grey to white, with some weathered darker patches; the chapel has a grey metal gable. — confidence: high — source: 1984 inventory; school aerial; Wikimedia Commons photograph (2009).
- Distinctive features:
  - Built July 1933 for the Jesuit St. Francis Xavier Novitiate on the former Paradise View / Paradise Farm tract; Portland firm Poole & McGonigle, with structural help from Dougan-Reverman. The chapel, begun after the main block, was designed by Lyle Bartholomew of Portland and was presented as a memorial to Barbara D'Arcy (historically St. Barbara Memorial Chapel).
  - The original exterior was plain concrete; a black waterproofing coat earned it the nickname “The Black Castle.” That appearance is historic only. In 1955, Seattle architect John Maloney directed a major remodelling: a cafeteria and two dormitory wings were added on the north, the chapel was connected to the main block, and the complex was unified in buff/orange brick. Planned exterior ornament and sculpture were never built, so the Art Deco character is severe and massing-based, not richly decorated.
  - The Jesuits sold the property in 1974; the Delphian School opened here in 1976. Eligible/contributing in the Oregon Historic Sites Database; not individually listed on the National Register (NR date: N/A).
  - Public uses of the building today (school's own pages, no personal detail): classrooms, dining room, chapel used as a performing-arts space, recreation room, foyer/art gallery, and dormitories in the wings (the school states that younger students live in the main building; older students also use separate halls such as Dragon Hall). A chapel restoration to preserve the stained-glass windows was announced by the school for 2025.
- Photos: see the section photos below; the best single overall view of the front and roofs is the school's aerial, [AerialCampusShot.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1733509476/delphian/pcpunwa0ykw5s135uuqu/AerialCampusShot.jpg). Historic approach photograph and a 1984 sketch plan are in the [inventory PDF, pages 2–3](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=19820.pdf).
- Notes for the game: Treat this as one connected complex with several clearly different volumes (1933 brick core and wings; chapel gable; lower kitchen/dining; cream 1955 dorm wings). CD-2 asks that the kitchen remain a separate lower volume — do that. The existing campus.js figure “north kitchen 48 × 11 m, 3.6 m walls” was not independently confirmed as a distinct 1-storey volume; the north rear *roof* I measured is about 50 × 17 m and includes the 3-storey dorm/dining block. Do not invent a height in metres. OpenStreetMap way 678519248 (`building=school`, about 3,690 m²) is a single simplified outline: it cuts notches for the chapel and courts, largely omits the north rear block, and (visually, against the same Esri image) sits about 5 m south-east of the actual roofs. Use it only as a rough shape, with that offset applied, or prefer a tracing from the imagery.

### Front facade and tower (CD-1a)
- What / where: The south-south-east front, the face seen from the loop drive and from the valley. This is the most-seen spot in the game (spawn / Long Walk start).
- Size: Four-storey core; three-storey wings set back and lower; tower about six storeys. Frontage on the order of 115–125 m (see overall size). Wing depth about 12–16 m. Tower and wall heights in metres: unknown. — confidence: high on storeys and rhythm; medium on plan metres — source: 1984 inventory; school aerial; Esri imagery.
- Materials & colours: Variegated buff to orange running-bond brick; slightly pink/cream cast-stone or precast details (sills, coping, tower face, entrance). Window frames are light; glass reads mid-grey. Windows are a mix of one-over-one double-hung sash and later aluminium casements (1984 inventory); many still read as simple vertically proportioned rectangles. — confidence: high on brick and stone locations; medium on exact current sash type for every opening — source: 1984 inventory; school aerial; 2018 entrance 360; 2009 Commons photograph.
- Window counts and rhythm (counted on the school's aerial, checked against the 2009 Commons view):
  - Four-storey core, west of the tower: **4 vertical bays**, grouped **2+2** with a wider brick pier in the middle; one rectangular window per bay per floor; **4 storeys** (16 windows). High confidence.
  - Four-storey core, east of the tower: the same **4 bays, 2+2 grouping, 4 storeys** (16 windows). The outer (eastern) pair of bays sits in a slightly set-back stepped mass, then the roofline drops to the three-storey wing. High confidence.
  - Tower south face: a tall cream/buff stone (or precast) panel recessed between wide brick corner piers. Near the top, **6 small square openings in 2 columns × 3 rows**. Below that the stone continues as a smooth shaft with horizontal joints, dropping into the entrance recess. High confidence.
  - East three-storey wing: an **even, not 2+2, grid** of single rectangular windows, **3 storeys**. Six bays are clear at the east end and two at the junction with the four-storey core; the same spacing continues behind three ornamental trees. Total about **12–14 bays per floor**. Confidence: medium-high (not every bay is unobstructed).
  - West three-storey wing: the same even 3-storey grid. At least **3–4 bays** are clear at the west end and several at the junction with the core; a large fir hides the middle. A similar total to the east wing is likely, but the exact count is not confirmed. Confidence: medium. West-end elevation (the short west wall) is mostly brick; small side openings may exist but were not clearly counted.
  - Spandrels: the windows sit in **recessed vertical channels between projecting brick piers**. Between storeys the spandrel is **inset brick of the same colour**, with a light sill (and a thin light lintel on some openings). They do **not** read as large contrasting stone panels. This matters for CD-1a's “stone spandrels” task: model light stone *sills* (and the stone tower/entrance/coping), not cream stone spandrel slabs.
  - Coping: a thin light-coloured (cast-stone or precast) cap along the parapets of the core and the wings. There is no heavy decorative cornice.
- Distinctive features:
  - The Art Deco character is in the **stepped massing** (3-storey wings < 4-storey core < tower) and the vertical piers, not in rich ornament. The 1984 inventory calls it a “rather severe form of Art Deco” and notes that planned ornament was never built.
  - The tower cap is a **plain rectangular parapet** with a slightly heavier top and thin coping — not an ornate multi-step ziggurat. From downhill the massing reads as stepped; from close below, the crown is a simple box.
  - A small brick chimney / stair or lift overrun sits on the four-storey roof just east of the tower; a rooftop antenna has appeared in older photos (2009). HVAC units sit on the core roof west of the tower.
  - The chapel's darker sloped roof peeks up just behind the tower in aerial views of the front.
- Photos:
  - School aerial (best front + roof + lawn + loop in one frame): [AerialCampusShot.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1733509476/delphian/pcpunwa0ykw5s135uuqu/AerialCampusShot.jpg) on the [Gallery](https://www.delphian.org/we-are-delphian/gallery) / [Tour](https://www.delphian.org/tour) pages.
  - 2018 360, node “Delphian School Entrance,” looking at the doors and up at the tower: [virtual tour](https://www.delphian.org/uploaded/tour/index.html) (tiles under `tiles/facade1/`).
  - Distant south view (27 August 2009, M.O. Stevens, CC BY 3.0): [File:The Delphian School in Sheridan, Oregon.JPG](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Delphian_School_in_Sheridan,_Oregon.JPG). Shows the full front, the six tower openings, and Larson Hall in the right foreground (a separate building, downhill to the south-east — do not merge it with the Main Building).
  - Historic road-approach photograph: inventory PDF, page 2.
- Notes for the game: Do not draw an even window grid on the four-storey core (that is the current game's weak spot). Use 2+2 bays either side of the tower, brick piers, inset brick spandrels, light sills, and a thin coping. Keep the outer two east bays of the core slightly set back. Fall back to today's simple windows only beyond about 250 m, as CD-1a specifies. sRGB vs linear colour: the brick is a warm variegated buff/orange, not a flat tan.

### Entrance, plaza and lettering (CD-1b)
- What / where: The south entrance, on the centre line of the four-storey core, under the tower. Reached from the loop drive by a raised brick plaza.
- Size: A short **upper flight of roughly 5–6 risers** from the brick plaza up to the doors, and a **shorter lower flight of roughly 3–4 risers** from the drive-side walk up onto the plaza. Exact riser counts are not surveyed (medium confidence, counted from the 2018 360 and the school aerial). Plaza width and stair width: unknown in metres. — source: [entrance 360](https://www.delphian.org/uploaded/tour/index.html); school aerial.
- Materials & colours:
  - Recessed portal in cream/buff stone or precast, framed by the tower's brick piers. No separate projecting canopy — the recess itself is the shelter (high confidence; clear in both the 2018 360 and the aerial).
  - Lettering **THE DELPHIAN SCHOOL** in two centred lines of dark capital letters on the stone panel immediately above the doors (high confidence, 2018 360 close view).
  - Doors: a dark recessed opening with what now appear to be glazed / metal-framed double doors. The 1984 inventory recorded “panelled oak with two lights in each door”; those historic leaves may have been replaced. Model the current recessed dark/glazed double doors unless the owner supplies a later close-up (medium confidence on current leaf type; high that they are double doors in a deep recess).
  - Steps are light grey concrete, some treads grooved; simple metal tube handrails sit on both sides of each flight. The “cheeks” are not tall stone parapets: the upper stair is set between the raised planted beds, and the lower stair is cut through a low curved tan terrace wall.
  - Plaza paving: red/brown brick with a distinctive **buff/cream geometric X (or large diamond) of lighter brick** on the field, seen from above in the aerial and from eye level in the 360. A low curved tan retaining / terrace wall wraps the planted mounds.
- Distinctive features:
  - Two large rounded evergreen shrubs (clipped mounds) flank the upper steps, in the raised beds.
  - Bowl-shaped light-grey planters with flowers sit on the plaza.
  - Metal slat benches (grey, contemporary park style) along the plaza sides.
  - **Lamp posts:** silver/grey metal poles with a wide circular disc shade (a flat “saucer” top). At least one pair flanks the upper entrance steps in the planted beds; the aerial also shows a matching post at the east edge of the plaza where the concrete walk begins. This is the campus lamp style to instance in CD-1b / CD-3b.
  - **Flagpoles:** at least two, in the planted area / lawn immediately in front of the entrance, flying the US flag and another blue flag (likely the state or school flag). The 2018 360 shows poles on the lawn among the trees to either side; the aerial shows poles at the plaza shrubs. Do not invent extra poles beyond the two (or possibly a close pair on the east side) that are actually visible.
- Photos:
  - Eye-level entrance, lettering, doors, steps, plaza pattern, lamps, benches, planters: [tour entrance 360](https://www.delphian.org/uploaded/tour/index.html) (`tiles/facade1/`).
  - Plaza, lower steps, terrace wall, lamps, walks and flags from above: [AerialCampusShot.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1733509476/delphian/pcpunwa0ykw5s135uuqu/AerialCampusShot.jpg).
- Notes for the game: The brief and PLAN.md mention a “canopy” — **do not add a projecting canopy**; it is a recessed stone portal. Lettering is two lines, THE DELPHIAN SCHOOL, on the stone above the doors, not a separate signboard. The plaza is raised, brick with a light X, and is drivable only if treated as a flat surface; the steps themselves are the solid obstacle (CD-1b default: steps and flagpole solid, lamp posts drive-through). Easy to get wrong: merging the two stair flights into one, or hiding the entrance behind a dark embankment (the CD-1c lawn bug).

### Front lawn and loop drive (CD-1b, CD-1c)
- What / where: The smooth lawn and curving asphalt drive on the south side of the front, between the facade and the downhill slope toward Larson Hall and the valley.
- Size: Not surveyed in metres. The lawn is wider and more open east of the entrance; west of the entrance the drive comes closer and a bay of perpendicular parking sits against the west wing. — confidence: high on layout from aerial and satellite; low on metre widths — source: school aerial; Esri World Imagery; OSM loop way 168629464 (use for topology, not exact width).
- Materials & colours: Smooth green lawn; light-grey concrete walks; asphalt drive with no yellow centre line (a campus road). A concrete ribbon / sidewalk runs along the building side of the drive. Some planting beds and mulch, and a reddish-brown planted or dry-grass strip on the downhill (south) side of the loop. White-painted perpendicular parking stalls on the asphalt in front of the west wing (about eight to ten stalls are visible on the satellite / aerial, but do not treat that count as surveyed).
- Distinctive features:
  - A **curved concrete walk** leaves the east side of the entrance plaza, crosses the front lawn, and continues around the east end of the building toward the courtyard and the gym. Other concrete paths run downhill toward Larson Hall (the two-storey brick building with a brownish roof, south-east of the Main Building, “across the street and down the stairs from [the] main school building” in the school's public orientation materials).
  - The loop is a curving one-way-looking campus drive, not a tight formal oval immediately against the doors; there is lawn and the raised plaza between the road and the facade.
  - Foundation plantings: three ornamental deciduous trees along the east wing, large rounded shrubs at the entrance, a large fir in front of the west wing, and smaller shrubs in mulch beds.
  - Short black path-bollard lights appear on the concrete walks around the east dorm / courtyard (see BoysDormExterior), in addition to the silver disc lamps at the plaza.
- Photos: [AerialCampusShot.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1733509476/delphian/pcpunwa0ykw5s135uuqu/AerialCampusShot.jpg) (best overall); entrance 360 faces toward the drive and valley; Esri imagery for stall layout and path traces.
- Notes for the game: CD-1c should flatten the jagged “teeth” and remove the dark road-embankment wall so this lawn meets the road cleanly, as in the aerial. Paths should be flat, light concrete, slightly above the grass, polygon-offset, and drivable. Do not invent kerbs (owner dislikes them) unless a later photo clearly shows one; the drive edge here reads as asphalt against grass or a low concrete ribbon.

### Courtyard (CD-2)
- What / where: The enclosed landscape on the north side of the front range, mainly east of the chapel: a paved lower plaza and a slightly raised lawn, with the chapel's east wall on one side, the north face of the front range and the cream 1955 dorm on the others, and the dining colonnade under the chapel.
- Size: Not surveyed as a single rectangle. The grassy eastern court is on the order of the gap between the chapel (about 18 m wide) and the east wing / east dorm; do not invent a metre figure. — confidence: high on the features present; low on overall metres — source: school courtyard stills and 2018 courtyard 360; Esri imagery; [BoysDormExterior.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1596478823/delphian/egpzs31okwwtjiyovjyg/BoysDormExterior.jpg).
- Materials & colours: Tan/beige rectangular concrete pavers on the lower plaza (running/stacked bond, not herringbone). A circular inset of smaller, browner pavers around the fountain, with a square metal drain nearby. Grass on the upper lawn. Cream/beige painted or stucco seat-walls with a thin buff-brick cap. Concrete walks on the lawn.
- Distinctive features:
  - **Fountain:** a small contemporary composition of rough-hewn stones (dark grey and rust/ochre) with water sheeting into a low circular wet area, not a classical tiered fountain. A low rectangular stone sits beside the upright slabs. One stone carries a short carved dedication (a family name). For a public game, **omit the personal name**; keep the stones.
  - **Fire pit and amphitheatre seat-wall:** a circular cream drum with a metal grate (star / wagon-wheel pattern) stands in front of a distinctive **three-tier curved cream seat-wall** (a small amphitheatre), which then continues as a single-height planter wall with a brick cap, wrapping in a wide arc around the paved court. Raised planting behind the wall: ornamental grasses, perennials, shrubs, a purple-leaf tree, and a lawn above.
  - **Fire stair:** a dark grey/black open-steel exterior stair, three storeys, attached to the end of the cream 1955 dorm wing. Intermediate landings, a mid-level balcony, then a straight flight down to a concrete pad. At the base: two small cream utility boxes (storage or services). Visible in the school's “East wing boys' dorm exterior” photo and the courtyard 360.
  - **Picnic tables:** a row of brown/bronze metal mesh picnic tables with attached benches under the dining colonnade, with bowl planters between some of them.
  - Concrete walks and short black bollard lights on the lawn side (east of the chapel / by the dorm).

### Chapel (CD-2)
- What / where: Projects north from the central core, east wall facing the grassy courtyard, west wall facing the paved service / kitchen court. Historically St. Barbara Memorial Chapel (Lyle Bartholomew, begun after the 1933 main block, connected to the main building in 1955). Now the school's “Chapel Performing Arts Space.”
- Size: Gable roof about **27 m long × 18 m wide** (medium confidence; satellite roof edges). Wall height in metres: unknown. The volume is a tall single-storey nave (pointed-arch windows are very tall) over, on the courtyard side, a one-storey glazed dining connection. — source: Esri imagery; 1984 inventory; school photos.
- Materials & colours: The same buff/orange brick as the main block. **Grey standing-seam (or ribbed) metal gable roof**, moderate pitch, simple gable ends, a small bump or vent at the north peak. Light coping / eave line and a gutter. Wooden roof trusses inside, “decorated with stencilled patterns” (1984 inventory) — visible in the school's chapel interior photograph (dark stained timber, decorative painting on the trusses and soffit). Interior walls cream/white with medium-brown wood wainscot; hanging lantern-style lights; wood pews; a raised stage at the north end. — confidence: high on exterior materials; high that interior trusses and stained glass exist; medium on roof-metre size — source: 1984 inventory; BoysDormExterior; courtyard 360; [ChapelPerformanceSpace.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1596478658/delphian/bjqhclxarsdymke2yrps/ChapelPerformanceSpace.jpg) (interior; the school's photo also shows a performance — use it for the room only).
- Window counts and rhythm:
  - East (courtyard) wall: a row of **tall pointed-arch stained-glass windows**, brick between them. The school's BoysDormExterior view of the full east wall, checked against the 2018 courtyard 360, shows about **six similar pointed arches plus a narrower lancet at the south end** (the 360 also shows a window air-conditioning unit in that right-hand lancet as of 2018). Confidence: medium-high on “six plus a narrower end lancet”; a close, square-on photo would lock the count.
  - North end (interior): **two** pointed-arch stained-glass windows in the end wall, inside a large pointed-arch opening, with smaller pointed-arch side openings flanking the stage.
  - Long walls, interior: at least three tall arches per side are visible in the view toward the stage; that is consistent with a six-bay long wall of which the camera does not show every bay.
  - Glass is leaded, with cooler blue/grey religious (historic) themes as described in 1984; the school said in 2025 that it was restoring these windows. For the game, keep coloured leaded pointed arches, not clear rectangular windows.
- Distinctive features: Pointed-arch bays on a brick Art Deco complex (a Gothic chapel attached to a 1933 Deco block) — easy to flatten into ordinary windows. The grey metal gable is the main “dark roof” seen from the air, just behind the tower.
- Photos: courtyard 360 (`tiles/courtyard/`, especially the chapel face); [BoysDormExterior.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1596478823/delphian/egpzs31okwwtjiyovjyg/BoysDormExterior.jpg); [ChapelPerformanceSpace.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1596478658/delphian/bjqhclxarsdymke2yrps/ChapelPerformanceSpace.jpg); tour node “Chapel Performing Arts Space” (`tiles/Chapel/`).
- Notes for the game: CD-2's “tall arched chapel windows” are **pointed (Gothic) arches with stained glass**, not round-headed or plain rectangles. Keep the chapel as its own gabled volume, connected but readable from the court and from the air.

### Kitchen and dining wing (CD-2)
- What / where: The 1955 cafeteria complex connected to the north end of the chapel, wrapping the chapel's north and west sides, with a glazed colonnade under the chapel's east wall looking onto the courtyard. The school's dining room is in this part of the building (wide windows; picnic tables outside).
- Size: One storey on the courtyard and service sides. Metre dimensions of this 1-storey wrap are not cleanly separated from adjoining roofs on the satellite: a low roof west of the chapel is on the order of roughly 28 × 17 m, but that is a rough roof patch, not a surveyed footprint (low-to-medium confidence). Wall height in metres: unknown. I could not confirm the existing campus.js figure of 48 × 11 m with 3.6 m walls as a distinct kitchen-only box; 48 m is close to the *north rear block* roof length (about 50 m), which is a deeper, partly 3-storey mass. — source: Esri imagery; courtyard 360; 1984 inventory (cafeteria added 1955).
- Materials & colours:
  - Courtyard / dining colonnade: cream/beige rectangular posts, a flat roof with a modest overhang and a light soffit, large rectangular glazed openings (dining looking onto the court), a thin darker band at the sill of the chapel wall above. White HVAC units sit on a flat roof behind/above (visible in Courtyard_03).
  - West / service side (courtyard 360, looking away from the chapel): a one-storey cream/yellow **panelised** wall, a distinctive **orange / terracotta fascia** along the eave, two or more wide **three-part windows** with white blinds, and a natural-wood door with a narrow transom under a slightly lower connecting roof. Low shrubs and an ornamental tree in mulch along this wall.
- Distinctive features: The orange fascia is an easy colour accent to miss. The colonnade is the everyday face of the dining room, with a row of picnic tables. A paved service court with parked cars lies west of the chapel and this wing (see parking side).
- Photos: courtyard 360 (`tiles/courtyard/` faces toward the chapel colonnade and toward the cream kitchen wall); Courtyard_03; tour node “Dining Room” (`tiles/Dining/`).
- Notes for the game: Keep this as a **low, separate volume** attached to the chapel and the north block, not as a 3- or 4-storey brick wing. CD-2's “kitchen wing loading dock” is almost certainly on the west service court; no clear street-level photo of a dock was found (see Gaps).

### 1955 dormitory wings (part of the Main Building, CD-2)
- What / where: Two dormitory wings added on the north in 1955 (Maloney). The school publicly labels an “East Dorm Wing” / “East wing boys' dorm exterior” and a “West Dormitory Wing.” The clearly photographed east addition is a cream, horizontally panelled three-storey block with the black fire stair, north-east of the chapel, forming one side of the grassy court. A similar lighter-roofed block is visible behind the west brick wing in the school aerial.
- Size: Three storeys (photographs). The north rear roof (which includes dining/dorm) is about 50 × 17 m (medium). Individual wing metres: unknown. — source: 1984 inventory; BoysDormExterior; school aerial; Esri imagery.
- Materials & colours: Cream / beige painted or panelised walls in horizontal registers, not the 1933 buff brick (a brick stair or end connector appears at one end of the east wing). Reddish tile or terracotta coping / eave. Regular large rectangular windows, light or pale-blue frames, several bays on the long side (an exact count was not locked; about eight to ten bays are visible on the long elevation in BoysDormExterior, medium confidence). A chimney near the fire-stair end of the east wing.
- Distinctive features: These wings should **not** be textured as Art Deco brick. The contrast of cream panels + black steel stair against the brick chapel is the courtyard's skyline.
- Photos: [BoysDormExterior.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1596478823/delphian/egpzs31okwwtjiyovjyg/BoysDormExterior.jpg); courtyard stills 01–02; school aerial (west addition behind the west brick wing).
- Notes for the game: The 1933 east and west *front* wings are brick, 3-storey, and face the lawn; the 1955 dorms are the cream north additions. Do not paint both types the same.

### Back, west wing and parking side (CD-2)
- What / where: The west and north-west sides of the complex, against the trees: a paved **service court** west of the chapel and kitchen (cars parked close to the building — the likely loading-dock / service yard), and a larger asphalt parking lot along the tree line with angled stalls (“rear drive + angled stalls” on the local annotated plan). A rear / back drive continues north through the trees (OSM way 678519216). The north face of the rear block looks onto this parking, not onto a second formal lawn.
- Size: Not surveyed in metres. — confidence: high that these paved courts and lots exist in the locations shown on Esri imagery; low on stall counts and door positions — source: Esri World Imagery; school aerial (west lot visible at left); OSM service drives.
- Materials & colours: Asphalt, painted stall lines, dense evergreen forest along the west edge. The west brick front-wing end and the cream 1955 block close this side. Rooftops on this side are more weathered (grey, patched).
- Distinctive features: This is the utilitarian side — parked cars, service access, tree wall — not a second formal facade. Door positions on the parking elevation were **not** clearly visible in any public street-level photograph found.
- Photos: Esri / satellite views (local working mosaic `esri_z19.png`); left edge of [AerialCampusShot.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1733509476/delphian/pcpunwa0ykw5s135uuqu/AerialCampusShot.jpg). No good close public photo of the west parking doors or a loading dock was found.
- Notes for the game: Do not copy the south front's stone entrance or 2+2 bay rhythm onto this side. Leave west-side doors schematic unless the owner supplies a photo. Keep drive-through access in the west service court and the north lot.

### Roof (CD-2)
- What / where: Roofs of the whole complex, visible from the air (jet / hill views in the game) and as the “dark roof patch” on the satellite.
- Size: See overall plan dimensions. Pitches: wings and core nearly flat (low parapet); chapel a moderate gable; no pitch angle was measured. — confidence: high on which volumes are flat vs gabled; low on degrees — source: school aerial; Esri imagery.
- Materials & colours:
  - Four-storey core and east front wing: light grey / white membrane, relatively clean, thin parapet, a few vents or stacks and at least one hatch.
  - West front wing: a clearly **darker, weathered grey/brown membrane** (the most obvious dark patch on the front half).
  - Chapel: grey metal gable, the dark sloped patch just behind the tower.
  - Kitchen / service roofs west of the chapel: dirty grey, crowded with white HVAC units.
  - North rear block: weathered white/grey membrane with rust-coloured stains and equipment.
- Distinctive features: Tower rising from the south edge of the core roof; brick chimney / overrun just east of the tower; HVAC packs west of the tower and on the kitchen and rear roofs; chapel gable peeking behind the tower; the dirty west-wing membrane vs the clean east-wing white roof. Skylights were not clearly identified as a separate class of object (some hatches or small vents only) — do not invent rows of skylights.
- Photos: [AerialCampusShot.jpg](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1733509476/delphian/pcpunwa0ykw5s135uuqu/AerialCampusShot.jpg) (best); Esri World Imagery close-up of the roofs.
- Notes for the game: CD-2's “dark roof patch on the satellite” is mainly **the chapel gable + the weathered west-wing and kitchen/rear membranes**, not a single painted rectangle on an otherwise uniform roof.

## Gaps — what you couldn't find, and what to ask the owner
- Height of the tower, core and wings in metres (no surveyed figure; storey counts only). A photographed person or a measured door in a square-on front photo would help, but no such measurement was made.
- Exact window count on the **west three-storey front wing** (large fir in every public front view) and a locked count for the east wing (12–14) and the chapel east wall (six arches + a narrower lancet).
- Exact stair riser counts, and a closer photo of the **current doors** if they have changed since the 2018 tour 360.
- Street-level photographs of the **west parking elevation**, any **loading dock**, and the **north rear** doors — the largest modelling gap for CD-2.
- World x,z of the building in the game (the docs copy used here does not include the baked world origin).
- Whether the 2025 chapel-window restoration changed the exterior (for example, removed the 2018 window AC unit).
- Confirmation that personal names on the courtyard fountain should stay out of the public game (recommended: yes).
- Most useful owner photos, in order: square-on **front and entrance close-up**; **courtyard** including the chapel wall; **back / kitchen / loading** side; west parking doors. (This matches the open questions already in CURSOR.md / PLAN.md.)
